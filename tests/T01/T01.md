## 1. Idea and references            (written BEFORE the run)

- **The idea in one sentence:** on an NSE index pair, the ratio of the two legs' prices drifts back toward its own trailing-year average after it has moved more than one standard deviation away from it — so holding only the leg that has lagged (the "cheap" leg) from the first week the ratio is beyond ±1 sd until the ratio is back at its mean earns a higher after-cost CAGR and a shallower drawdown than holding the same legs for the same lengths of time at random moments.
- **Where it came from:** the review of 2026-11-12 — the desk's "±1 sd mean reversion" idea, framed as a band on the ratio chart (mean ± 1 sd) rather than a z-score; a hunch from looking at the B10b ratio charts with their 52-week moving average.
- **The decision this will change:** whether a ratio-band rule goes into the design of the asset-allocation sleeves (as a way to tilt between size indices), or the idea is closed and the sleeve work stays on the supertrend / gold switch.
- **What is already known:**

| ref | what it says | relationship |
|---|---|---|
| wm-BREAK §6 pair cards (`6_pair_coint`) | no index pair has a fixed level: ADF p ≥ 0.43 for 9 of 10 pairs; half-lives > 52 weeks | the ratio has no level to revert to on the whole history; a trailing band re-centres on the last year, which this test relies on |
| wm-BREAK B10b (cont.), 2026-11-04 | inside each era the richest-fifth weeks of the ratio were followed by a worse quarter than the cheapest-fifth weeks, after COVID and in 2024–26, not before COVID; the within-era quintile is known only in hindsight | suggests some within-regime pull-back; not point-in-time |
| wm-BREAK B10c (standalone), 2026-11-05 | the ratio's last-4-week move continues rather than reverses in 9 of 10 pairs; a 4-week reversal rule loses in every index pair | the opposite sign at short horizons |
| z-score long-only switch (standalone), 2026-11-05 | entry at ±2 z (trailing 52 weeks), exit at 0.5, long-only: trails 50/50 in all 10 pairs net; the fitted-hedge-ratio spread version is ±0 across settings | the closest prior test; exploratory, no null, no pre-registration |
| wm-BREAK §7 pair-trading rule | long/short at |z| = 2, exit 0.5: "did not work" on every pair net | long/short form of the same bet |

- **What this test is NOT asking:** whether a long/short pairs book on these indices makes money (it is long-only); whether the ratio is cointegrated or has a long-run level (it uses a trailing band, so it only asks whether a one-year deviation closes); whether the band predicts *returns* of either leg on its own; anything about gold timing (the gold pairs are context).

## 2. Hypothesis and pre-registered criteria   (BEFORE)

- **What I already saw:** the exploratory z-score switch (entry ±2, exit 0.5, 26-week cap) on the same data trailed 50/50 in every pair net, and the per-trade relative return had a positive median but a negative mean (the long trades that hit the cap lost most). I have NOT looked at the ±1 sd / exit-at-mean rule on this data. The expected result, given the above, is that H1 fails; the test is written so that it can pass.
- **Hypotheses:**
  - **H1 (timing):** the rule's after-cost CAGR beats random timing with the same switches and exposure (null N-A) at the 95th percentile, and its max drawdown is shallower than N-A's median, in at least 4 of the 6 index pairs. *Falsified if* fewer than 4 of 6 pairs reach the 95th percentile on CAGR, or fewer than 4 of 6 have a shallower drawdown than the N-A median.
  - **H2 (leg choice):** given the rule's own timing, holding the cheap leg beats holding a randomly chosen leg (null N-B) at the 95th percentile on after-cost CAGR in at least 4 of the 6 index pairs. *Falsified if* fewer than 4 of 6.
- **Null table** (Appendix B format) — **N-A decides**:

| Dimension | Randomised / held / not matched | How | Measured in 4.0 as |
|---|---|---|---|
| *when the sleeve is in a leg* (the ingredient) | randomised (N-A) | the real decision sequence is cut into trade spells (leg, length in weeks) and the neutral gaps between them; each draw permutes the order of the trade spells among themselves and the order of the gaps among themselves, then rebuilds the target sequence over the same decision weeks; 2,000 draws; seed `default_rng([pair_id, exit_id, draw_id])` | — |
| *which leg is held* (the ingredient of H2) | randomised (N-B) | the real timing kept; each spell's leg replaced by a fair coin flip; 2,000 draws; seed `default_rng([100 + pair_id, exit_id, draw_id])` | — |
| pair, calendar, decision weeks, fill rule, cost | held | constant | — |
| number of trade spells, each spell's length, weeks in leg a / leg b / neutral, number of switches | held (N-A, N-B) | by construction of the permutation / flip | counts, strategy vs every draw: must be identical |
| the legs' own trend (a draw may sit in a leg during a different market regime) | not matched | — | volatility and drawdown of the draws vs the strategy, shown |
| the neutral book's drift (50/50 bought at the time of entering neutral, then unrebalanced) | not matched | — | shown as the 50/50 comparators |

- **Statistic, direction, why:** after-cost CAGR of the daily-valued sleeve, higher is better (the return the sleeve earns); max drawdown of the daily NAV, shallower is better (the risk the sleeve carries). Both per pair. CAGR answers "did it earn more", drawdown answers "did it hurt less"; a timing rule can win one by losing the other.
- **Pass mark and number of draws:** 95th percentile of 2,000 draws for CAGR (p ≤ 0.05); drawdown shallower than the null median. Percentile = (draws worse + 0.5 × ties) ÷ draws × 100; p = (1 + draws at least as good) ÷ (draws + 1); edge = strategy − null median.
- **Validity gates:**
  - **V1** the independent verifier (`verify_ratio.py`, no engine import) reproduces every pair-setting's final NAV, CAGR and max drawdown within 1e-9, and fails on a deliberately corrupted copy (a flipped decision; a scaled NAV).
  - **V2** no look-ahead: the engine asserts for every decision that the band uses only decision weeks ≤ t and that the trade date is strictly after the decision date; check count > 0, violations = 0.
  - **V3** the null held what it promised: for every draw, the number of spells, the weeks in a / b / neutral and the number of switches equal the strategy's.
  - **V4** regression: the "always neutral" sleeve (50/50 bought at the first trade, never traded again) equals the closed-form NAV from the two legs' prices within 1e-9; after any code change the previous run's summary is reproduced exactly.
- **Reportability gate:** a pair-setting with fewer than 4 round trips or fewer than 200 decision weeks is **NOT REPORTABLE**: no number from it is quoted, and it counts as *not passing* in the 4-of-6 counts.
- **Pass criteria:**
  - **C1** after-cost CAGR ≥ the 95th percentile of N-A (2,000 draws) in ≥ 4 of the 6 index pairs, exit E1, window 52, cost 20.8 bps.
  - **C2** max drawdown shallower (less negative) than the N-A median in ≥ 4 of 6, same setting.
  - **C3** after-cost CAGR ≥ the 95th percentile of N-B in ≥ 4 of 6, same setting.
  - H1 passes iff C1 and C2; H2 passes iff C3.
- **Robustness checks:**
  - **R1** costs 0 / 20.8 / 50 bps round trip (the decisions do not change; the numbers and the null do).
  - **R2** band window 26 and 104 weeks (52 is the reference, chosen as the middle of the grid, not by result).
  - **R3** exit E2 — hold until the ratio reaches the opposite band (or 26 weeks) — run through the same criteria; **E1 is the pre-named deciding exit**, E2 is reported.
  - **R4** sub-periods, descriptive only (no null): 2006–2015, 2016–2019, 2020–2023, 2024–2026 — CAGR and drawdown of the sleeve vs 50/50 and vs each leg.
  - **R5** the 4 gold pairs through the same code, reported, never counted.
- **Multiplicity:** decision settings tried per pair = 2 exits × 3 windows = 6; with 3 cost levels, 18 numbers per pair; 10 pairs. The reference setting (E1, 52, 20.8) is fixed above. Any claim about the best-looking non-reference setting is judged against the 98th percentile of its own null, and the number of settings tried is stated beside it.
- **Reference setting:** exit E1, window 52 weeks, cost 20.8 bps, max hold 26 weeks — chosen by position (the middle window; the exit the hypothesis names; the desk's cost), never by result.
- **The MDE:** 2.8 × the standard deviation of the N-A CAGR distribution for that pair (80% power, 5% two-sided), computed by analyse.py and printed beside every FAIL as "undetectable below X pp a year".
- **Descriptive only (no claims):** the per-trade "extra" (the held leg's fill-to-fill return minus the peer's over the hold); the share of trades positive; the band charts; the gold pairs; the sub-periods; the number of touches that were not traded because the sleeve was already in a trade.
- **Outcome map:**

| pattern | conclusion |
|---|---|
| C1, C2, C3 pass | the rule times AND picks the leg: PROVISIONAL (one window, no hold-out); next step a hold-out on 2027 data and a cost sweep |
| C1 and C2 pass, C3 fails | the band marks *times* (regimes) but not *which leg*: a volatility-regime effect, not mean reversion; re-test as a timing-only rule |
| C1 fails, C3 passes | the leg choice is right but the entries are no better than random moments: the information is in "which leg is cheap", not in the ±1 sd touch; re-test with a different trigger |
| C1 fails and C3 fails | REFUTED at the MDE for this band and exit; E2 and the windows reported for completeness; the idea is closed unless E2 passes on the pre-named criteria, which would make it PROVISIONAL at best (E2 was not pre-named) |

## 3. Methodology                     (BEFORE)

- **Data table:**

| file | contributes | through-date | review verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| data/index_ohlcv_all.csv (nifty100, midcap100, smallcap100, microcap250) | daily OHLC: the signal (close), the fill (OHLC4), the valuation (close) | 2026-08-28 | PASS WITH NOTES |
| data/MAUc1_daily.csv | daily OHLC of the gold leg (gold pairs only) | 2026-09-08 | PASS WITH NOTES |

- **Construction, step by step:**
  1. Each leg's daily bars come through `analysis/approved_data.load_daily()` (fingerprint checked; the two loader rules H1/H2 of the data review applied; blank closes dropped).
  2. A pair's **calendar** is the intersection of its two legs' trading days. Nothing is carried forward.
  3. **Decision days** are the last calendar day of each ISO week (a Friday unless Friday is a holiday for either leg). The **trade day** for a decision is the next calendar day.
  4. The **ratio** is lr_t = ln(P_a,t ÷ P_b,t) on decision-day closes. The **band** at decision week t is the mean and standard deviation (ddof 1) of lr over the trailing W = 52 decision weeks ending at and including t, requiring at least 42 of them; before that there is no band and no decision.
  5. **State** is one of neutral / A (100% in leg a) / B (100% in leg b). From neutral: if lr_t < mean − sd and lr_{t−1} was not below mean − sd (the first week of a run beyond the lower band) → A; if lr_t > mean + sd and the week before was not above → B. A touch while already in a trade is ignored (counted). **Exit E1:** in A, exit when lr_t ≥ mean_t; in B, when lr_t ≤ mean_t; or after 26 decision weeks in the trade. **Exit E2:** in A, exit when lr_t > mean_t + sd_t; in B, when lr_t < mean_t − sd_t; or after 26 weeks. An exit and an entry on the same decision week are both taken (the sleeve passes straight from A to B when the ratio jumps across the band).
  6. **The book:** NAV starts at 1.0. On the first decision week that has a band the sleeve buys 50/50 by value on the trade day; the first valuation is the calendar day *before* that trade day, so the first trade's cost is inside the measured path. On a switch the sleeve sells what it must and buys the target on the trade day at each leg's `fill` price (OHLC4, or the close on a stale-OHL row); the cost is 10.4 bps of the value traded on each side. Neutral = 50/50 by value at the moment it is entered, then unrebalanced until the next switch. Units are held; NAV every calendar day = Σ units × close. No cash, no financing, no slippage beyond the flat cost.
  7. **Statistics** per pair-setting-series: CAGR = NAV_end^(365.25 ÷ days) − 1 over the valuation window; max drawdown = min over days of NAV ÷ running max − 1; annualised vol = sd of daily log returns × √252; final NAV; round trips; weeks in A / B / neutral; switches.
  8. **Comparators:** hold a; hold b; 50/50 bought at the first trade and never rebalanced; 50/50 rebalanced every decision week at the fill, with cost (constant mix); "always neutral" = the sleeve with no trades (equals comparator 3 — gate V4).
  9. **Null N-A:** the strategy's decision sequence as spells and gaps (step 5) → permuted as in the null table → the same book code (step 6). **Null N-B:** the same spells, legs replaced by coin flips → the same book code.
  10. Everything is written to `evidence/<run>/`: `summary.csv` (one row per pair × setting × series), `draws_<pair>_<exit>.csv` (every draw's CAGR, drawdown, counts), `decisions.csv` (one row per decision week: date, lr, mean, sd, state before, state after, reason), `trades.csv` (one row per trade: decision date, trade date, from, to, fills, value traded, cost; the per-trade extra), `nav_<pair>_<setting>.csv` (daily), `audit.json` (fingerprints, rules, seeds, PIT check count and violations, stale-row count).
- **How the test works, in plain words:** take N100 ÷ SC100. Every Friday close we look at ln(N100 ÷ SC100) against its last-52-week average and spread. The first Friday it is more than one spread below the average, N100 has lagged SC100 unusually: on Monday the sleeve sells its SC100 and holds only N100. It keeps N100 until the first Friday the ratio is back at its average (E1), or 26 weeks, then goes back to 50/50 on the Monday. Each trade costs 10.4 bps of what is traded. The sleeve's value is marked every day. To see whether *when* it did this mattered, we take the same number of trades, with the same legs and the same lengths, and scatter them at random over the same weeks — 2,000 times — and ask how often the random scatter earned more and fell less. To see whether *which leg* mattered, we keep the real timing and flip a coin for the leg, 2,000 times.
- **Point-in-time rule:** the band at decision week t is computed from the series truncated at t (the engine passes `lr.iloc[:i+1]` and asserts its last index equals the decision date); the fill is taken on the next calendar day and the engine asserts `trade_date > decision_date`; every assertion is counted in audit.json (gate V2).
- **Execution and cost:** decide at the decision day's close; trade the next calendar day at `fill` (OHLC4); 10.4 bps per side on value traded; no financing; no slippage model beyond that; no tax.
- **Effective sample size and MDE:** the independent unit is a round trip; the z-score switch on the same pairs produced 12–14 per full-history pair in 10 years, so 15–30 per index pair are expected on 20 years — thin; the MDE from the null's spread (2.8 × sd of the N-A CAGR) is reported next to every FAIL and is expected to be of the order of several pp a year.
- **Known contaminants:**

| issue | handling |
|---|---|
| price indices, no dividends | both legs alike; affects levels, not the relative test; stated |
| gold front-month continuation (roll inside the return; 2026-01-30 open item) | gold pairs reported, never counted |
| Micro250 from 2022 only | its three pairs are reportable only if ≥ 4 round trips; the short window is stated beside them |
| overlapping windows in the band | none in the decision unit (round trips do not overlap); the null draws carry the same structure |
| the 2008 crash inside the index-pair window | a sub-period in R4 |
| no hold-out | 10–20 years, 10 pairs, a first test; sub-periods (R4) stand in; a true hold-out (data after 2026-09) is the first next step |

### Added after the run (2026-11-12)

Differences between the locked text (§2–§3) and what ran, all recorded in `audit.json` or the engine's docstrings; none changes a criterion:

1. **N-A permutes blocks, not single spells.** §3 step 5 lets an exit and an entry fall on the same decision week, so two trade spells can abut with a zero-length gap. Permuting single spells among spell positions and gaps among gap positions would create and destroy such abutments in the draws, and the number of switches would not be held. The engine therefore permutes *maximal runs of abutting spells* (blocks) among the block positions and the *non-zero* gaps among the non-zero-gap positions (a zero gap can only sit at the two ends and stays there). V3 then checks every count of every draw against the strategy's: 400,000 of 400,000 kept them. For the deciding setting (W52, E1) the difference is immaterial — 3 zero gaps among the 133 trade spells of the six index pairs (`python tests/T01/null_blocks.py`); under E2 it is not (237 zero gaps among 537 spells), which is why the block version was needed. See F-RA-M01.
2. **N-B flips each block with one coin**, not each spell, so that the alternation inside a block (A straight to B) is kept and the counts with it.
3. **Seed formula has a fourth element.** The nulls were run at three windows, so the seed is `default_rng([pair_id (+100 for N-B), exit_id, draw_id, window])`; the locked text had three elements because it named one window. Deterministic either way.
4. **Loader rule H3** (a close with blank open/high/low: fill = close; three gold rows) was added to `approved_data.py` after the lock and before the run (commit d0601a6), because `5050_rebal` for the gold pairs produced a NaN at 2021-10-04 on a dry run. §3 step 1 names H1/H2 only. `data/DATA_REVIEW.md` lists H3.
5. **File names** carry the window and cost: `draws_<pair>_<W>_<exit>_<cost>.csv`, `nav_<pair>_<W>_<exit>_<cost>.csv`; the per-trade extra is in its own file `trade_extras.csv` rather than a column of `trades.csv`.
6. **Nulls were run** at every window and exit at the reference cost, and at every cost on the reference window and both exits (100 pair-settings × 2 nulls × 2,000 draws = 400,000 draws), so that 4.4 and 4.5 can rank every setting that was tried.
