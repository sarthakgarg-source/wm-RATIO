## 1. Idea and references            (written BEFORE the run)

- **The idea in one sentence:** on an NSE index pair, from the first week the plain price ratio P_a ÷ P_b is more than one standard deviation away from its trailing-52-week mean, hold only the lagging leg until the ratio is back at its mean — however long that takes — and this earns a higher after-cost CAGR and a shallower drawdown than the same trades at random moments.
- **Where it came from:** the desk's "±1 sd mean reversion" idea, framed as a band on the ratio chart (mean ± 1 sd), on the plain ratio as the desk asked on 2026-11-13, and with every trade held until its exit rule fires as the desk asked on 2026-11-17 (tests/T03/PLAN.md).
- **The decision this will change:** whether the ratio-band switch is worth any further work. If it fails, the ±1 sd first-touch switch with exit at the mean is closed, and the only open door in the family is a pre-registered change to the entry or the exit (§7). If it passes, it is PROVISIONAL at most (the rule was settled after results on the same data had been seen) and a hold-out decides.
- **What is already known:**

| ref | what it says | relationship |
|---|---|---|
| T02 | the same rule judged at the 95th percentile, rerun on 2026-11-17 with this design | the same rule |
| earlier versions of this rule (removed from the repository on 2026-11-17 at the desk's request; kept in git under the tag `archive/2026-11-17-earlier-versions`) | a band on ln(P_a ÷ P_b), and a version with a time limit on each trade, both failed every criterion on this data; per trade the held leg beat its peer more often than not, but a few long losing trades outweighed the many small winners | why the expected result is a fail |
| E01 (exploratory) | at the 13–52-week horizons, no index pair's ratio reverses more than a random walk's; MC100 ÷ SC100 continues at 8–13 weeks; the gold pairs continue at most horizons; a trailing 52-week z is beyond ±1 in more than half the weeks even on a random walk | argues that long holds meet continuation, not reversion |
| E03 (exploratory) | the cheaper leg does not catch up more than on a random walk; trades that end within a few months mostly win, trades held over a year all lost; faster, deeper entries went with better trades | the same picture trade by trade |
| the mechanics of exit E1 | the exit compares the ratio with its *trailing* mean, which moves toward the ratio; a trade against a persistent trend still ends — when the trend pauses and the trailing mean catches up — not only when the ratio swings back | why holding on need not turn losers into winners |

- **What this test is NOT asking:** a time limit or a stop on the trades; another band width; the log ratio; an entry filter (depth, speed, market state); the reverse orientation (b ÷ a); a long/short book.

## 2. Hypothesis and pre-registered criteria   (BEFORE)

- **What I already saw:** **this test ran on 2026-11-17 under an earlier text of these sections** (the same rule, nulls, seeds and criteria; the earlier text also compared the rule with a version that had a time limit and with the log-ratio version). On 2026-11-17, after that run, the desk asked for both earlier versions to be removed from the repository, and §1–§3 were rewritten without them and re-locked; the run's evidence is re-analysed with this text, not rerun, because nothing that produces the evidence changed. Its result is therefore known: C1 0 of 6, C2 2 of 6, C3 0 of 6. Also seen before this design: the earlier versions in the table above (they failed every criterion); E01 (the ratios' cycles and the z-score of the ratio against random walks), E02 (hindsight segmentation: long stretches in both directions) and E03 (the legs' forward returns by z bucket, and every trade of this rule with the market's condition at its entry). The expected result is that H1 and H2 fail. The test is written so that it can pass.
- **Hypotheses:**
  - **H1 (timing):** the rule's after-cost CAGR beats random timing with the same switches and exposure (null N-A) at the 98th percentile, and its max drawdown is shallower than N-A's median, in at least 4 of the 6 index pairs. *Falsified if* fewer than 4 of 6 pairs reach the 98th percentile on CAGR, or fewer than 4 of 6 have a shallower drawdown than the N-A median.
  - **H2 (leg choice):** given the rule's own timing, holding the cheap leg beats holding a randomly chosen leg (null N-B) at the 98th percentile on after-cost CAGR in at least 4 of the 6 index pairs. *Falsified if* fewer than 4 of 6.
- **Null table** (Appendix B format) — **N-A decides**:

| Dimension | Randomised / held / not matched | How | Measured in 4.0 as |
|---|---|---|---|
| *when the sleeve is in a leg* (the ingredient of H1) | randomised (N-A) | the real decision sequence is cut into blocks (maximal runs of abutting trade spells, e.g. A for 5 weeks straight into B for 3) and the neutral gaps between them; each draw permutes the blocks among the block positions and the non-zero gaps among the non-zero-gap positions (a zero gap can only sit at either end and stays there), then rebuilds the target sequence over the same decision weeks; 2,000 draws; seed `default_rng([pair_id, exit_id, draw_id, window])` | — |
| *which leg is held* (the ingredient of H2) | randomised (N-B) | the real timing kept; each block's legs flipped together by one fair coin (A ↔ B), which keeps the alternation inside a block; 2,000 draws; seed `default_rng([100 + pair_id, exit_id, draw_id, window])` | — |
| pair, calendar, decision weeks, fill rule, cost | held | constant | — |
| number of trade spells, each spell's length, number of switches, weeks neutral; weeks in leg a / leg b (N-A) or in a trade (N-B) | held | by construction of the permutation / flip | counts, strategy vs every draw: must be identical |
| the legs' own trend (a draw may sit in a leg during a different market regime) | not matched | — | volatility and drawdown of the draws vs the strategy, shown |
| the neutral book's drift (50/50 bought at the time of entering neutral, then unrebalanced) | not matched | — | shown as the 50/50 comparators |

- **Statistic, direction, why:** after-cost CAGR of the daily-valued sleeve, higher is better (the return the sleeve earns); max drawdown of the daily NAV, shallower is better (the risk the sleeve carries). Both per pair.
- **Pass mark and number of draws:** the **98th percentile** of 2,000 draws for CAGR (p ≤ 0.02), raised from the 95th because the rule was settled after results on the same data had been seen; drawdown shallower than the null median. Percentile = (draws worse + 0.5 × ties) ÷ draws × 100; p = (1 + draws at least as good) ÷ (draws + 1); edge = strategy − null median.
- **Validity gates:**
  - **V1** the independent verifier (`verify_ratio.py`; no engine import; the plain ratio and the absence of a maximum hold come from this design, not from the engine) re-derives every decision and reproduces every pair-setting's final NAV, CAGR and max drawdown within 1e-9; it fails if audit.json names another form or a maximum hold; and it fails on a deliberately corrupted copy (a flipped decision; a scaled NAV).
  - **V2** no look-ahead: the engine asserts for every decision that the band uses only decision weeks ≤ t and that the trade date is strictly after the decision date; check count > 0, violations = 0.
  - **V3** the null held what it promised: for every draw, the counts in the null table equal the strategy's.
  - **V4** regression: (a) the "always neutral" sleeve equals the closed-form 50/50 buy-and-hold NAV within 1e-9; (b) the same rule run as T02 (tests/T02/evidence/headline/) makes the same decision in every pair, window, exit and week, and its strategy rows (final NAV, CAGR, max drawdown at every cost) equal this run's within 1e-9.
- **Reportability gate:** a pair-setting with fewer than 4 round trips or fewer than 200 decision weeks is **NOT REPORTABLE**: no number from it is quoted, and it counts as *not passing* in the 4-of-6 counts.
- **Pass criteria:**
  - **C1** after-cost CAGR ≥ the 98th percentile of N-A (2,000 draws) in ≥ 4 of the 6 index pairs, exit E1, window 52, cost 20.8 bps, no maximum hold.
  - **C2** max drawdown shallower (less negative) than the N-A median in ≥ 4 of 6, same setting.
  - **C3** after-cost CAGR ≥ the 98th percentile of N-B in ≥ 4 of 6, same setting.
  - H1 passes iff C1 and C2; H2 passes iff C3.
- **Robustness checks:**
  - **R1** costs 0 / 20.8 / 50 bps round trip (the decisions do not change; the numbers and the null do).
  - **R2** band window 26 and 104 weeks (52 is the reference, the middle of the grid).
  - **R3** exit E2 — hold until the ratio reaches the opposite band — through the same criteria; **E1 is the deciding exit**, E2 is reported.
  - **R4** sub-periods, descriptive only (no null): 2006–2015, 2016–2019, 2020–2023, 2024–2026 — CAGR and drawdown of the sleeve vs 50/50.
  - **R5** the 4 gold pairs through the same code, reported, never counted.
- **Multiplicity:** 2 exits × 3 windows = 6 decision settings per pair, 3 cost levels, 10 pairs; the reference setting is fixed; a claim about the best-looking non-reference setting is judged at the 99th percentile of its own null, with the number of settings beside it. Across versions: this rule was settled after earlier versions had been run on the same data; any pass is PROVISIONAL at most until a hold-out on data after 2026-09-08 confirms it.
- **Reference setting:** exit E1, window 52 weeks, cost 20.8 bps, no maximum hold — chosen by position (the middle of the window grid, the first exit), never by result.
- **The MDE:** 2.8 × the standard deviation of the N-A CAGR distribution for that pair (80% power, 5% two-sided), computed by analyse.py and printed beside every FAIL as "undetectable below X pp a year".
- **Descriptive only (no claims):** the per-trade "extra" (the held leg's fill-to-fill return minus the peer's over the hold); the share of trades positive; the gold pairs; the sub-periods; the touches not traded because the sleeve was already in a trade.
- **Outcome map:**

| pattern | conclusion |
|---|---|
| C1, C2, C3 pass | the band times AND picks the leg: PROVISIONAL (settled after earlier versions on the same data, no hold-out); a hold-out on data after 2026-09-08 decides |

## 3. Methodology                     (BEFORE)

- **Data table:**

| file | contributes | through-date | review verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| data/index_ohlcv_all.csv (nifty100, midcap100, smallcap100, microcap250) | daily OHLC: the signal (close), the fill (OHLC4), the valuation (close) | 2026-08-28 | PASS WITH NOTES |
| data/MAUc1_daily.csv | daily OHLC of the gold leg (gold pairs only) | 2026-09-08 | PASS WITH NOTES |

- **Construction, step by step:**
  1. Each leg's daily bars come through `analysis/approved_data.load_daily()` (fingerprint checked; the loader rules H1–H3 of the data review applied; blank closes dropped).
  2. A pair's **calendar** is the intersection of its two legs' trading days. Nothing is carried forward.
  3. **Decision days** are the last calendar day of each ISO week (a Friday unless Friday is a holiday for either leg). The **trade day** for a decision is the next calendar day.
  4. The **ratio** is r_t = P_a,t ÷ P_b,t on decision-day closes, not logged, where a is the pair's first asset in the order N100, MC100, SC100, Micro250, GoldMCX (N100 ÷ MC100, …, Micro250 ÷ GoldMCX). The **band** at decision week t is the mean and standard deviation (ddof 1) of r over the trailing W = 52 decision weeks ending at and including t, requiring at least 42 of them; before that there is no band and no decision.
  5. **State** is one of neutral / A (100% in leg a) / B (100% in leg b). From neutral: if r_t < mean_t − sd_t and the week before had a band and r_{t−1} was not below its band (the first week of a run beyond the lower band) → A; if r_t > mean_t + sd_t and the week before had a band and was not above it → B. A touch while already in a trade is ignored (counted). **Exit E1:** in A, exit when r_t ≥ mean_t; in B, when r_t ≤ mean_t. **Exit E2:** in A, exit when r_t > mean_t + sd_t; in B, when r_t < mean_t − sd_t. **There is no maximum hold**: a trade ends only at its exit rule. An exit and an entry on the same decision week are both taken (the sleeve passes straight from A to B when the ratio jumps across the band). A trade still open on the last decision week stays open; the book values it to the last day and the per-trade table marks it open.
  6. **The book:** NAV starts at 1.0. On the first decision week that has a band the sleeve buys 50/50 by value on the trade day; the first valuation is the calendar day *before* that trade day, so the first trade's cost is inside the measured path. On a switch the sleeve sells what it must and buys the target on the trade day at each leg's `fill` price (OHLC4, or the close on a stale-OHL row); the cost is 10.4 bps of the value traded on each side. Neutral = 50/50 by value at the moment it is entered, then unrebalanced until the next switch. Units are held; NAV every calendar day = Σ units × close. No cash, no financing, no slippage beyond the flat cost.
  7. **Statistics** per pair-setting-series: CAGR = NAV_end^(365.25 ÷ days) − 1 over the valuation window; max drawdown = min over days of NAV ÷ running max − 1; annualised vol = sd of daily log returns × √252; final NAV; round trips; weeks in A / B / neutral; switches.
  8. **Comparators:** hold a; hold b; 50/50 bought at the first trade and never rebalanced; 50/50 rebalanced every decision week at the fill, with cost (constant mix); "always neutral" = the sleeve with no trades (equals comparator 3 — gate V4a).
  9. **Null N-A:** the strategy's decision sequence as blocks and gaps (null table) → permuted → the same book code (step 6). **Null N-B:** the same blocks, each block's legs flipped by one coin → the same book code. Both run at every window and exit at the reference cost, and at every cost on the reference window.
  10. Everything is written to `evidence/<run>/`: `summary.csv` (one row per pair × window × exit × cost × series), `draws_<pair>_<W>_<exit>_<cost>.csv` (every draw's CAGR, drawdown, counts, counts_ok), `decisions.csv` (one row per decision week: date, `ratio` = r_t, mean, sd, position, state before, state after, reason), `trades.csv`, `trade_extras.csv` (the per-trade extra), `nav_<pair>_<W>_<exit>_<cost>.csv` (daily; regenerated by run.sh, not kept in git), `audit.json` (test, ratio = plain and its formula, fingerprints, rules, seeds, PIT check count and violations, null counts, stale-row count), `verify.log`.
- **How the test works, in plain words:** take N100 ÷ SC100, the plain ratio of the two index levels. Every Friday close we compare it with its last-52-week average and spread. The first Friday it is more than one spread below the average, N100 has lagged SC100 unusually: on Monday the sleeve sells its SC100 and holds only N100. It keeps N100 until the first Friday the ratio is back at its average (E1), however long that takes, then goes back to 50/50 on the Monday. Each trade costs 10.4 bps of what is traded, and the sleeve is marked every day. To see whether *when* mattered, the same trades (same legs, same lengths, abutting trades kept together) are scattered at random over the same weeks 2,000 times; to see whether *which leg* mattered, the real timing is kept and a coin picks the leg, 2,000 times.
- **Point-in-time rule:** the band at decision week t is computed from the weekly series truncated at t (the engine passes `.iloc[:i+1]` and asserts its last index equals the decision date); the fill is taken on the next calendar day and the engine asserts `trade_date > decision_date`; every assertion is counted in audit.json (gate V2).
- **Execution and cost:** decide at the decision day's close; trade the next calendar day at `fill` (OHLC4); 10.4 bps per side on value traded; no financing; no slippage model beyond that; no tax.
- **Effective sample size and MDE:** the independent unit is a round trip — of the order of 25–35 on the 20-year index pairs and 5–8 on the Micro250 pairs at the reference setting; the MDE (2.8 × sd of the N-A CAGR) is reported next to every FAIL and is of the order of several pp a year.
- **Known contaminants:**

| issue | handling |
|---|---|
| price indices, no dividends | both legs alike; affects levels, not the relative test; stated |
| gold front-month continuation (roll inside the return; 2026-01-30 open item) | gold pairs reported, never counted |
| Micro250 from 2022 only | its three pairs are reportable only if ≥ 4 round trips and ≥ 200 decision weeks; the short window is stated beside them |
| orientation | fixed before the run (step 4); the reverse orientation is not run and is listed in §6 as not tested |
| the rule was settled after earlier versions had been run on the same data | C1 and C3 at the 98th percentile; any pass is PROVISIONAL; a hold-out on data after 2026-09-08 is the real test |
| long holds: a trade can last years, and one can be open at the end | the book values it to the last day; the per-trade table marks it open |
| an E1 trade ends when the trailing mean reaches the ratio, with or without reversion | this is the rule under test, not a defect |
| the 2008 crash inside the index-pair window | a sub-period in R4 |
| no hold-out | a hold-out on data after 2026-09 is the first next step if anything passes |
