## 1. Idea and references            (written BEFORE the run)

- **The idea in one sentence:** T02's rule — on an NSE index pair, from the first week the plain price ratio P_a ÷ P_b is more than one standard deviation away from its trailing-52-week mean, hold only the lagging leg until the ratio is back at its mean — **with no 26-week cap**: the trade lasts until its exit rule fires, however long that takes; the claim is that this earns a higher after-cost CAGR and a shallower drawdown than the same trades at random moments.
- **Where it came from:** the request of 2026-11-17, after T02 and the exploratory study E03: "can we remove the 26 week rule then?" and "yes please do and remove the 26 week rule" (tests/T03/PLAN.md). E03 had shown, at T02's reference setting, that nearly every trade that exited at the mean beat its peer and nearly every trade that ran to the 26-week cap lost.
- **The decision this will change:** whether the 26-week cap is what sinks the ratio-band switch. If T03 also fails, removing the cap does not rescue it: the ±1 sd first-touch switch with exit at the mean is closed capped and uncapped, and the only open door in the family is a pre-registered entry filter (T02.md §7). If T03 passes, the pass rests on a change suggested by results seen on the same data, so it is PROVISIONAL at most and a hold-out decides (see Multiplicity and the outcome map).
- **What is already known:**

| ref | what it says | relationship |
|---|---|---|
| T02 F-RA-S03 (REFUTED), 2026-11-17 | the same rule with the 26-week cap: C1 0 of 6, C2 2 of 6, C3 0 of 6; CAGR below the N-A median in 5 of 6 index pairs; MDE 2.5–11.4 pp/yr | the test this one repeats with one ingredient changed |
| T01 F-RA-S01 (REFUTED), F-RA-S02 (PROVISIONAL) | the log form fails the same way; per trade a positive median extra but a negative mean: a few large losers | the per-trade pattern T03 is meant to change |
| T02 F-RA-M02 | the plain and the log band make the same decision in at least 95.9% of weeks on every pair | T03 keeps the plain form; the log form would give nearly the same answer |
| E03 (exploratory, 2026-11-17), exit reasons at T02's reference setting | index pairs: 82 trades exited at the mean, 94% beat their peer, median extra +3.2%; 48 ran to the cap, 2% beat their peer, mean extra −9.0%, median −6.6%. Gold pairs: 36 at the mean (100% good, mean +9.1%); 29 at the cap (7% good, mean −22.4%) | why the cap is suspected; but a capped trade is one in which the ratio was still moving the wrong way after 26 weeks |
| the mechanics of exit E1 | the exit compares the ratio with its *trailing* mean, which moves toward the ratio; without a cap a trade against a persistent trend still ends — when the trend pauses and the trailing mean catches up — not only when the ratio swings back | why removing the cap need not turn the capped losers into winners |
| E01 (exploratory) | at the 13–52-week horizons, no index pair's ratio reverses more than a random walk's; MC100 ÷ SC100 continues at 8–13 weeks; the gold pairs continue at most horizons | argues that longer holds meet continuation, not reversion |

- **What this test is NOT asking:** any other cap (13 or 52 weeks), a stop-loss at the far band, another band width, the log form, an entry filter (depth, speed, market state), a long/short book; anything T02 did not ask except the cap.

## 2. Hypothesis and pre-registered criteria   (BEFORE)

- **What I already saw:** all of T01 and T02 (both REFUTED: C1 0 of 6, C2 2 of 6, C3 0 of 6; per trade a positive median extra and a negative mean). All of E01 (the ratios' cycles against random walks, and the z-score of the ratio: no swing-back beyond a random walk's at 13–52 weeks; turning points near z = ±2, as on random walks), E02 (hindsight segmentation: long stretches in both directions) and E03: forward returns of the cheaper and the richer leg by z bucket (the cheaper leg does not catch up more than on a random walk), and every T01/T02 trade at the reference setting with the market's condition at its entry — the exit-reason split in §1, and that faster, deeper entries went with better trades. E03 measured the capped trades only up to the cap: **what those trades did after week 26 has not been looked at.** Before the lock, the engine edit for T03 (the cap as a per-test constant, TEST_MAX_HOLD) was checked by rerunning T01 and T02 with 0 draws: all 600 summary rows of each and their decisions, trades and extras reproduced exactly. In the same check the verifier was given the wrong cap on purpose, to see that it fails; its failure lines printed one number of the uncapped rule: SC100 ÷ Micro250, exit E2, window 52, 50 bps, final NAV 2.140 against 2.150 with the cap — a non-deciding setting. No other number of the uncapped rule has been read. The code paths of T03 were run once before the lock with 3 draws into a throw-away folder (the engine, the two regression runs, the verifier, analyse.py, report.py, check_page.py) to check that they run; all exited 0; their output was sent to the null device and deleted unread, and only the exit codes were read. The expected result, given E01 and the mechanics of exit E1, is that H1 and H2 fail again: the losers that the cap cut at 26 weeks run on until the trailing mean catches up, with losses of about the same size or larger. The test is written so that it can pass.
- **Hypotheses:**
  - **H1 (timing):** with no maximum hold, the rule's after-cost CAGR beats random timing with the same switches and exposure (null N-A) at the 98th percentile, and its max drawdown is shallower than N-A's median, in at least 4 of the 6 index pairs. *Falsified if* fewer than 4 of 6 pairs reach the 98th percentile on CAGR, or fewer than 4 of 6 have a shallower drawdown than the N-A median.
  - **H2 (leg choice):** given the rule's own timing, holding the cheap leg beats holding a randomly chosen leg (null N-B) at the 98th percentile on after-cost CAGR in at least 4 of the 6 index pairs. *Falsified if* fewer than 4 of 6.
- **Null table** (Appendix B format) — **N-A decides**:

| Dimension | Randomised / held / not matched | How | Measured in 4.0 as |
|---|---|---|---|
| *when the sleeve is in a leg* (the ingredient of H1) | randomised (N-A) | the real decision sequence is cut into blocks (maximal runs of abutting trade spells) and the neutral gaps between them; each draw permutes the blocks among the block positions and the non-zero gaps among the non-zero-gap positions (a zero gap can only sit at either end and stays there), then rebuilds the target sequence over the same decision weeks; 2,000 draws; seed `default_rng([pair_id, exit_id, draw_id, window])` | — |
| *which leg is held* (the ingredient of H2) | randomised (N-B) | the real timing kept; each block's legs flipped together by one fair coin (A ↔ B); 2,000 draws; seed `default_rng([100 + pair_id, exit_id, draw_id, window])` | — |
| pair, calendar, decision weeks, fill rule, cost | held | constant | — |
| number of trade spells, each spell's length, number of switches, weeks neutral; weeks in leg a / leg b (N-A) or in a trade (N-B) | held | by construction of the permutation / flip | counts, strategy vs every draw: must be identical |
| the legs' own trend (a draw may sit in a leg during a different market regime) | not matched | — | volatility and drawdown of the draws vs the strategy, shown |
| the neutral book's drift (50/50 bought at the time of entering neutral, then unrebalanced) | not matched | — | shown as the 50/50 comparators |

- **Statistic, direction, why:** as T01 and T02 — after-cost CAGR of the daily-valued sleeve, higher is better (the return the sleeve earns); max drawdown of the daily NAV, shallower is better (the risk the sleeve carries). Both per pair.
- **Pass mark and number of draws:** the **98th percentile** of 2,000 draws for CAGR (p ≤ 0.02), raised from T01/T02's 95th because the change under test was suggested by results seen on the same data (E03, T02); drawdown shallower than the null median. Percentile = (draws worse + 0.5 × ties) ÷ draws × 100; p = (1 + draws at least as good) ÷ (draws + 1); edge = strategy − null median.
- **Validity gates:**
  - **V1** the independent verifier (`verify_ratio.py --ratio plain --max-hold none`; no engine import; the form and the cap come from this design through run.sh, not from the engine) re-derives every decision on P_a ÷ P_b with no cap and reproduces every pair-setting's final NAV, CAGR and max drawdown within 1e-9; it fails if audit.json names any other form or cap; and it fails on a deliberately corrupted copy (a flipped decision; a scaled NAV).
  - **V2** no look-ahead: the engine asserts for every decision that the band uses only decision weeks ≤ t and that the trade date is strictly after the decision date; check count > 0, violations = 0.
  - **V3** the null held what it promised: for every draw, the counts in the null table equal the strategy's.
  - **V4** regression: (a) the "always neutral" sleeve equals the closed-form 50/50 buy-and-hold NAV within 1e-9; (b) the engine as changed for T03, run with T02's settings (`--test T02`, 0 draws), reproduces every row of T02's `evidence/headline/summary.csv` — final NAV, CAGR and max drawdown — within 1e-9; (c) the same with T01's settings against T01's `evidence/headline/summary.csv`.

  - H1 passes iff C1 and C2; H2 passes iff C3.

  - **R3** exit E2 — hold until the ratio reaches the opposite band, with no cap either — through the same criteria; **E1 is the deciding exit**, E2 is reported.
  - **R4** sub-periods, descriptive only (no null): 2006–2015, 2016–2019, 2020–2023, 2024–2026 — CAGR and drawdown of the sleeve vs 50/50.

## 3. Methodology                     (BEFORE)

- **Data table:**

| file | contributes | through-date | review verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| data/index_ohlcv_all.csv (nifty100, midcap100, smallcap100, microcap250) | daily OHLC: the signal (close), the fill (OHLC4), the valuation (close) | 2026-08-28 | PASS WITH NOTES |
| data/MAUc1_daily.csv | daily OHLC of the gold leg (gold pairs only) | 2026-09-08 | PASS WITH NOTES |

- **Construction, step by step** (T02 §3 with step 5 changed):
  1. Each leg's daily bars come through `analysis/approved_data.load_daily()` (fingerprint checked; the loader rules H1–H3 of the data review applied; blank closes dropped).
  2. A pair's **calendar** is the intersection of its two legs' trading days. Nothing is carried forward.
  3. **Decision days** are the last calendar day of each ISO week (a Friday unless Friday is a holiday for either leg). The **trade day** for a decision is the next calendar day.
  4. The **ratio** is r_t = P_a,t ÷ P_b,t on decision-day closes, not logged, where a is the pair's first asset in the order N100, MC100, SC100, Micro250, GoldMCX (T01's and T02's orientation). The **band** at decision week t is the mean and standard deviation (ddof 1) of r over the trailing W = 52 decision weeks ending at and including t, requiring at least 42 of them; before that there is no band and no decision.
  5. **State** is one of neutral / A (100% in leg a) / B (100% in leg b). From neutral: if r_t < mean_t − sd_t and the week before had a band and r_{t−1} was not below its band (the first week of a run beyond the lower band) → A; if r_t > mean_t + sd_t and the week before had a band and was not above it → B. A touch while already in a trade is ignored (counted). **Exit E1:** in A, exit when r_t ≥ mean_t; in B, when r_t ≤ mean_t. **Exit E2:** in A, exit when r_t > mean_t + sd_t; in B, when r_t < mean_t − sd_t. **There is no maximum hold** — the only change from T02, whose trades also ended after 26 decision weeks. An exit and an entry on the same decision week are both taken. A trade still open on the last decision week stays open; the book values it to the last day and the per-trade table marks it open.
  6. **The book:** as T02 — NAV starts at 1.0; on the first decision week that has a band the sleeve buys 50/50 by value on the trade day; the first valuation is the calendar day before that trade day, so the first trade's cost is inside the measured path. On a switch the sleeve sells what it must and buys the target on the trade day at each leg's `fill` price (OHLC4, or the close on a stale-OHL row); the cost is 10.4 bps of the value traded on each side. Neutral = 50/50 by value at the moment it is entered, then unrebalanced until the next switch. Units are held; NAV every calendar day = Σ units × close. No cash, no financing, no slippage beyond the flat cost.
  7. **Statistics** per pair-setting-series: CAGR = NAV_end^(365.25 ÷ days) − 1 over the valuation window; max drawdown = min over days of NAV ÷ running max − 1; annualised vol = sd of daily log returns × √252; final NAV; round trips; weeks in A / B / neutral; switches.
  8. **Comparators:** hold a; hold b; 50/50 bought at the first trade and never rebalanced; 50/50 rebalanced every decision week at the fill, with cost; "always neutral" = the sleeve with no trades (equals comparator 3 — gate V4a). None depends on the cap, so they equal T02's.
  9. **Null N-A:** the strategy's decision sequence as blocks and gaps (null table) → permuted → the same book code (step 6). **Null N-B:** the same blocks, each block's legs flipped by one coin → the same book code. Both at every window and exit at the reference cost, and at every cost on the reference window.
  10. Everything is written to `evidence/<run>/` as T02 (`summary.csv`, `draws_<pair>_<W>_<exit>_<cost>.csv`, `decisions.csv`, `trades.csv`, `trade_extras.csv`, `nav_*.csv` regenerated by run.sh and not kept in git, `audit.json` with test = T03, ratio = plain, max_hold_weeks = null, `verify.log`), plus `t02_regression/` and `t01_regression/` for gates V4b and V4c.
- **How the test works, in plain words:** take N100 ÷ SC100, the plain ratio of the two index levels. Every Friday close we compare it with its last-52-week average and spread. The first Friday it is more than one spread below the average, N100 has lagged SC100 unusually: on Monday the sleeve sells its SC100 and holds only N100. It keeps N100 until the first Friday the ratio is back at its average (E1) — **with no time limit**; T02 gave up after 26 weeks — then goes back to 50/50 on the Monday. Each trade costs 10.4 bps of what is traded, and the sleeve is marked every day. To see whether *when* mattered, the same trades are scattered at random over the same weeks 2,000 times; to see whether *which leg* mattered, the real timing is kept and a coin picks the leg, 2,000 times.
- **Point-in-time rule:** as T02: the band at decision week t is computed from the weekly series truncated at t (the engine asserts it); the fill is taken on the next calendar day (asserted); every assertion is counted in audit.json (gate V2).
- **Execution and cost:** decide at the decision day's close; trade the next calendar day at `fill` (OHLC4); 10.4 bps per side on value traded; no financing; no slippage model beyond that; no tax.
- **Effective sample size and MDE:** the independent unit is a round trip. Without the cap the trades last longer and touches during a trade are ignored, so there will be fewer round trips than T02's 35–43 on the 20-year index pairs and 6–9 on the Micro250 pairs; the Micro250 pairs may fall below the reportability gate of 4. The MDE is reported beside every FAIL and is expected to be of the order of T02's 2.5–11.4 pp a year or larger.
- **Known contaminants:**

| issue | handling |
|---|---|
| price indices, no dividends | both legs alike; affects levels, not the relative test; stated |
| gold front-month continuation (roll inside the return; 2026-01-30 open item) | gold pairs reported, never counted |
| Micro250 from 2022 only | its three pairs are reportable only if ≥ 4 round trips and ≥ 200 decision weeks; the short window is stated beside them |
| the change was suggested by results seen on the same data (E03, T02) | C1 and C3 at the 98th percentile; any pass is PROVISIONAL; a hold-out on data after 2026-09-08 is the real test |
| long holds: a trade can last years, and one can be open at the end | the book values it to the last day; the per-trade table marks it open; the T03-vs-T02 table counts the open trades |
| an uncapped E1 trade ends when the trailing mean reaches the ratio, with or without reversion | this is the rule under test, not a defect; the capped-trades table shows how long those trades ran and what they earned |
| the null's blocks get longer when spells do | the counts are held exactly (V3); with fewer, longer blocks there are fewer distinct timings, stated beside the percentiles if a pair has fewer than 4 blocks |
| the 2008 crash inside the index-pair window | a sub-period in R4 |
| no hold-out | as T01 and T02 |
