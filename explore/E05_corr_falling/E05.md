# E05 — Does the ratio switch do better when the legs' correlation is falling as z reaches its extreme? (EXPLORATORY)

**Status:** EXPLORATORY — description of the past, not a finding, not a test of any rule. A filter that looks good here
is a candidate for a new pre-registered test (the next free T number, T04), never a rule on its own. Run after T02 and
T03 were locked, run and refuted (2026-11-17).
**Asked:** 2026-11-18 — "In the z score study, i am trying to see how the study performs when the correlation between
the pair is falling and z score reaches its defined extreme. add this in a different html. with all the controles of z
and corr window".
**Choices made with the desk on 2026-11-18:** "falling" = the correlation on the signal week is lower than N weeks
earlier, with N a control (4, 13 or 26 weeks); the extreme is a control (z beyond ±1, ±1.5 or ±2); shown: the rule's
trades split by falling / not falling, and the rule that takes an entry only when the correlation is falling, against
50/50 and against the unfiltered rule.
**Script:** `python explore/E05_corr_falling/falling.py` → `explore/E05_corr_falling/out/`
**Data:** the approved data of 2026-11-12, only through `analysis/approved_data.load_daily()`, on each pair's common
calendar and decision weeks (`analysis.ratio_engine.pair_frame`): index pairs from 2006-01-02, gold pairs from
2015-01-12, Micro250 pairs from 2022-01-10, to 2026-08-28.

## The controls (all on one page)

| control | options | default (chosen by position, not by result) |
|---|---|---|
| pair | the ten pairs | N100 ÷ SC100 (the other pages' default) |
| z window | 52, 26, 13 weeks (weekly closes); 250, 120, 60, 20 days (daily closes, read at each decision day) | 52 weeks (T03's) |
| z extreme | ±1, ±1.5, ±2 | ±1 (T03's) |
| correlation period | 13, 26, 52 weeks (weekly log returns on decision weeks); 20, 60, 120, 250 days (daily log returns) | 52 weeks (E03's default) |
| falling over | 4, 13, 26 decision weeks | 13 weeks (the middle) |

## The rule (Rule R with a chosen extreme)

z = (ratio − trailing mean) ÷ trailing sd over the chosen z window (E01's, `explore/E01_ratio_cycles/zwin.py`), read at the
decision day's close (the last trading day of the ISO week). From 50/50: the first decision week z crosses below
−extreme (it was not below it the week before) → hold 100% of the first leg; the first week it crosses above +extreme
→ hold 100% of the second leg. Exit when z is back through 0 (≥ 0 holding the first leg, ≤ 0 holding the second) → back
to 50/50, unrebalanced. Crosses during a trade are ignored; no maximum hold. Trade the next session at `fill` (OHLC4),
10.4 bps per side, units held, valued daily — through the engine's own book
(`explore/E01_ratio_cycles/variants.py`, which calls `analysis.ratio_engine.book`). With the 52-week window and ±1 this
is T03 exactly (checked against T03's evidence on every pair).

## "Correlation falling"

The legs' trailing Pearson correlation for the chosen period (E03's series: `corr_frame` in
`explore/E03_trade_conditions/conditions.py`; full window required; daily periods read at the decision day) on the
signal week, compared with its value N decision weeks earlier. **Falling** = lower now than N weeks ago; **not falling**
= equal or higher; **unknown** = either value missing (early in the data). Only data up to the signal week enters.

## What is shown

1. **The rule's trades, split.** Every closed trade of the unfiltered rule at the chosen z window and extreme, labelled by
   the correlation at its entry decision. Per group (falling / not falling): trades, share won (extra > 0), mean and
   median extra (the held leg's fill-to-fill return minus the peer's, no cost — E03's measure), median weeks held, share
   held over 52 weeks. Pooled over the six index pairs, and over the four gold pairs; per pair as well.
   **Chance check:** within each pair the falling / not-falling labels are shuffled among that pair's trades 2,000 times
   (seed `default_rng([107, z_window_id, extreme_id, corr_period_id, falling_over_id, pool_id])`); the percentile of the
   real difference (falling − not falling) in mean extra and in share won among the shuffled ones. Notable = ≤ 5 or ≥ 95.
   A group with fewer than 5 trades gets no percentile.
2. **The filtered rule.** The same rule, but an entry is taken only if the correlation is falling on that week (an
   unknown counts as not falling); and, for contrast, only if it is not falling. Skipping an entry can change the later
   trades (the sleeve stays 50/50, so a later cross can open a trade), so the filtered rule runs through the same state
   machine and book, not as a subset of trades. Per pair: closed trades, share won, CAGR, excess CAGR over 50/50
   bought once (E04's definition), max drawdown; the strategy ÷ 50/50 over time (month-ends), for the unfiltered and the
   falling-only rule. The six index pairs: in how many the falling-only rule beats the unfiltered one.
3. **Every setting at a glance.** Heat maps of the chance-check percentile across all correlation periods × N (for the
   chosen z window and extreme) and across all z windows × extremes (for the chosen correlation period and N), and of the
   filtered rule's count of index pairs beating the unfiltered rule — so one good-looking cell can be seen next to its
   neighbours.

## Checks (counted in `out/audit.json`; the script stops on a failure)

- The unfiltered rule at 52 weeks and ±1 equals T03's decisions and NAV on every pair (`variants.check_against_t03`).
- An entry filter that allows every week gives exactly the unfiltered rule (every pair, window and extreme).
- The unfiltered rule's excess CAGR at ±1 equals E04's (`explore/E04_signal_strength/out/outperformance_windows.csv`) for
  every pair and window.
- The correlations pass E03's look-ahead check (recomputed from the history cut at 12 dates per pair).

## Limits

- 7 z windows × 3 extremes × 7 correlation periods × 3 lookbacks = 441 settings per pair, two statistics, two pools:
  about one percentile in ten is notable by chance alone, and the settings overlap heavily. The page counts the notable
  ones against that expectation; a single cell is not a result.
- Falling vs not falling splits few trades in two: 4–35 closed trades per index pair at ±1 and 52 weeks, fewer at ±2.
- The chance check asks only whether the label goes with better trades; it says nothing about whether a filtered rule
  would beat random timing — that is a new pre-registered test.
- The filtered rule's results have no null here; they are descriptive.
- Gold: futures continuation with an unconfirmed roll on 2026-01-30 (F-RA-D-OPEN-2); gold pairs from 2015, Micro250 pairs
  from 2022 (short; few trades).

## Added after the first run (2026-11-18)

Everything above was written before the first run and is unchanged. A first run with 50 shuffles (to check the code)
showed two things that change how the page is read, so two summaries were added before the full run:

1. **Falling-only against not-falling-only, head to head.** The rule with every entry trails 50/50 on every index pair,
   so any version that trades less tends to beat it, whatever the filter: at the default setting the not-falling-only
   rule beat the every-entry rule by more than the falling-only rule did. Comparing falling-only with the every-entry rule
   alone would credit the correlation for what skipping trades does. The filtered-rule heat map now shows, per setting,
   in how many of the six index pairs falling-only beats not-falling-only (excess CAGR), with the comparisons against
   every entry in the hover; the results file and the page give all three counts over the 441 settings.
2. **Which way the notable split percentiles point.** The count against chance is now broken down by pool and direction
   (falling notably worse vs notably better, mean extra), because the notable cells were not split evenly between the two
   directions. The settings overlap, so these counts describe the grid, not independent results.

## How to read it (2026-11-18)

Every number is in `out/E05_results.md` and on the page, printed by the script.

1. **In the index pairs, a falling correlation at entry goes with worse trades, not better.** Across the 441 settings the
   mean extra of the falling-correlation trades is notably lower than the others' in 112 and notably higher in 8. It is
   clearest with the long z windows and the deeper extremes. At 52 weeks: at ±1 the falling trades won 57% against 69%;
   at ±1.5, 53% against 73% (mean extra −4.5% against −0.2%, percentile 0.6); at ±2, 50% against 77% (−4.8% against
   +2.4%, percentile 0.6). The 250-day window looks the same. With the short windows (13 weeks, 60 and 20 days) there is no
   clear difference. Falling-correlation trades also last longer: at 52 weeks and ±1, 30% are held over a year against 16%.
   Read plainly: when the two indices start moving apart while the ratio is already stretched, the gap tends to keep
   widening, which fits E04's finding that stretched ratios tend to stretch further.
2. **The gold pairs lean the other way** (65 settings notably better, 39 worse), on few trades (34 known at the default
   setting) and with the gold leg's unconfirmed 2026-01-30 roll; not something to lean on.
3. **As a filter, falling correlation is the wrong way round.** Head to head, the rule entering only on falling
   correlation does worse than the rule entering only when it is not falling in at least 4 of the 6 index pairs in 259 of
   441 settings, and better in 71. Both filtered versions often beat the rule with every entry, because trading less helps
   a rule that trails 50/50 — that is not the correlation's doing. Against 50/50 bought once, over all 2,646 (setting,
   index pair) cases, the rule with every entry is ahead in 84, the falling-only rule in 313 and the not-falling-only rule
   in 647: none of them is a way to beat holding both indices.
4. **Checks.** The unfiltered rule at 52 weeks and ±1 is T03 on every pair; an allow-everything filter is the unfiltered
   rule everywhere; the unfiltered excess at ±1 equals E04's (to 1e-16); the correlations pass the cut-history check
   (120 cuts, 0 violations). The page was stepped through all 441 settings for two pairs in a headless browser and every
   number it showed (22,050 checks) matched the CSVs.
5. **What would come next, if anything:** the opposite idea — skip entries while the correlation is falling, or prefer
   them when it is rising — is a new rule and would need its own pre-registered test (T04) with a hold-out; this page
   chose nothing and proves nothing.

## Added on 2026-11-19: equity curves against the asset classes (section 3 of the page)

**Asked:** 2026-11-19 — "plot the equity curves of the strategy in e05 which can be compared with different asset
classes". Everything above is unchanged, and so are sections 1–2 of the page: after the rerun their CSVs are
byte-identical.

- **What is drawn.** The value of 1, day by day, for the rule's three versions at the setting chosen at the top (every
  entry, entries only on falling correlation, entries only when not falling — the same runs as section 2, after costs),
  for 50/50 bought once, and for each approved asset bought once and held: N100, MC100, SC100, Micro250 and gold. These
  are all the asset classes in the approved data (Indian equity by size, and gold). There is no cash or bond series, and
  adding one needs the data owner and a new review. Also drawn: the drawdown from the running peak, and a table with the
  growth of 1, CAGR, volatility (daily log returns × √252) and max drawdown of each line.
- **Start.** The rule's first day (the first decision week with a z, which depends on the z window). Where they come
  later, also gold's first day (2015-01-12) and Micro250's (2022-01-10), so that those assets can be compared. A later
  start measures the same portfolios from that day: 1 on the pair's first day on or after the date.
- **Bought and held.** 1.0 on the rule's first day, bought at the asset's next session's fill with 10.4 bps (as the
  engine's `hold_a` / `hold_b`), valued at its own closes up to the pair's last day. The rule and 50/50 are valued on the
  pair's common days.
- **How the page draws it.** The statistics are the script's (`out/equity_summary.csv`: every curve, setting and start).
  The curves are replayed in the page from the embedded prices and the rule's weekly positions, using the engine's book
  formulas. Checks:
  - The script stops unless each pair's calendar rebuilds exactly from the page's calendar.
  - It also stops unless every curve's CAGR from the rule's first day equals section 2's (largest difference 2.2e-16).
  - In a headless browser, the page replayed all 17,871 (curve, setting, start) rows against the script's
    full-precision numbers (`--equity-check`). Growth and max drawdown matched exactly, CAGR to 2e-16 and volatility to
    4e-15.
  - The page shows any mismatch in red under the chart.
- **Caveats.** The indices are price indices without dividends, which understates every equity line against gold. Gold
  is a futures continuation with an unconfirmed roll on 2026-01-30 (F-RA-D-OPEN-2). Each curve is one path over one
  history, with no null: a description, not a finding.

**How to read it (2026-11-19; numbers from `out/E05_results.md` §6, default setting).**

1. **The rule's curves sit with the equity indices, not above them.** From the rule's first day (2006-10-20) to
   2026-08-28, on the three long index pairs the rule with every entry made 10.5%, 7.6% and 11.3% a year. 50/50 made
   12.5%, 10.3% and 12.4%, and MC100 alone 14.0%. Their max drawdowns (−65% to −77%) are as deep as the indices' own
   (−62% to −77%): the switch did not soften 2008 or 2020.
2. **From gold's first day (2015-01-12), gold beat every other line.** Gold made 16.3% a year with a −22% max
   drawdown. The equity indices made 10.0%–15.0% with −38% to −65%, and every version of the rule on the three long index
   pairs made 7.7%–13.3%.
3. **On the gold pairs, 50/50 of the index and gold was the better portfolio.**
   - The three older gold pairs, from 2015-10-30: 50/50 made 14.9%–16.8% with −20% to −25% drawdowns. The rule's
     versions made 6.9%–14.3%, eight of the nine with deeper drawdowns.
   - Micro250 ÷ gold, from 2022-10-28: 50/50 made 30.4%, the rule's versions 21.1%–25.7%.

   Switching everything into one leg gave up the diversification that holding both provides.
