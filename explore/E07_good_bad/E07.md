# E07 — Good and bad trades: what tells them apart, and can the rule be managed better? (EXPLORATORY)

**Status:** EXPLORATORY — description of the past, not a finding, not a test of any rule. Anything that looks useful
here is a candidate for a new pre-registered test (the next free T number, T04) with a true hold-out (data after
2026-09-08), never a rule on its own.
**Asked:** 2026-11-19 — "lets try to do some more things naa and try to find conditions when our z score rule will work.
we can analyse our both good and bad trades and try to find something from that".
**Script:** `python explore/E07_good_bad/goodbad.py` → `explore/E07_good_bad/out/`
**Data:** the approved data of 2026-11-12, only through `analysis/approved_data.load_daily()`, on each pair's common
calendar and decision weeks (`analysis.ratio_engine.pair_frame`): index pairs from 2006-01-02, gold pairs from 2015-01-12,
Micro250 pairs from 2022-01-10, to 2026-08-28.

## What is already known (E03–E06) and what is new here

E06 searched 24 conditions at entry and 7,211 rules built from them. Its main findings: a trade's outcome is almost
fixed by how long it is held (held 13 weeks or less: 77% worked; over 52 weeks: 1%), and the only rules that held up
after 2016 rest on a strong market in a few bull years. A year-by-year look (2026-11-19, not saved) found the rule beats
50/50 in years when the ratio moves little and loses in years when it trends. E07 looks at four things E06 did not:

1. **The ratio's character before entry.** How straight or how back-and-forth the ratio has been moving — the dimension
   the year-by-year look points at — measured at the entry decision with six new measures.
2. **The trade's path after entry.** E06 searched nothing during a trade. Here: how good and bad trades differ week by
   week after entry, whether a bad trade can be seen early, and two exits that act on it (a stop on z, a time stop).
3. **Waiting for the turn.** Entering when z starts coming back instead of at the first touch of the band.
4. **An honest test of predictability.** Models trained only on trades already closed, predicting the next year's
   trades, scored against shuffled labels and used as an entry filter against random skipping.

## The trades

Rule R (T03's rule, extreme ±1, exit when z is back through 0, no maximum hold) on every z window of the dropdowns (52,
26, 13 weeks on weekly closes; 250, 120, 60, 20 days on daily closes read at the decision day), all ten pairs, through
the engine's book (`explore/E01_ratio_cycles/variants.py`). They must be E06's trades exactly (same entries, exits and
extras; `explore/E06_trade_search/out/trades_all.csv`), with E06's 24 conditions at entry (recomputed with E06's own
functions and checked equal). **Good** = extra > 0 (the held leg's fill-to-fill return minus the peer's, before costs);
**bad** = extra ≤ 0. Index pairs decide what is said; gold pairs are reported separately.

## 1. Good and bad trades side by side

Per z window, index pairs pooled (and gold pairs): the path of z and of the held leg minus the peer (from the entry
fill, at each decision close) over the 52 decision weeks after the entry decision — median and quartiles, good trades
against bad. Both series exist whether or not the trade is still open, so the paths show what the market did after the
signal; the label is the trade's actual outcome. Also: weeks held, |z| at entry, the deepest |z| reached while open.

## 2. The ratio's character at entry — six new measures

All from the ratio's decision-week closes up to the entry decision (cut-history check: recomputed from the history cut
at 12 dates per pair, must equal the full computation up to each cut):

| key | measure |
|---|---|
| eff26, eff52 | path efficiency: \|net log change\| ÷ sum of \|weekly log changes\| over 26 / 52 weeks (1 = a straight line, near 0 = back and forth) |
| cross104 | how many times z (the chosen window) changed sign in the last 104 decision weeks |
| ac104 | lag-1 autocorrelation of the ratio's weekly log changes over 104 weeks (positive = moves continue, negative = moves reverse) |
| vr104 | variance ratio over 104 weeks: variance of 4-week log changes ÷ (4 × variance of weekly ones) (> 1 trending, < 1 mean-reverting) |
| r2_52 | R² of a straight line fitted to the log ratio over 52 weeks |
| hl104 | mean-reversion half-life in weeks, from an AR(1) fit of the log ratio on its previous week over 104 weeks; 520 when there is no reversion, capped at 520 |

Statistics as E06's (`uni` in `explore/E06_trade_search/search.py`): the rank correlation of each measure with the extra,
both ranked within each pair, against 2,000 shuffles of the extras within each pair (seed `default_rng([120, window_id,
measure_id])`), per window and all windows pooled (pair × window as the group), before 2016 and from 2016, and the gold
pairs. Notable = percentile ≤ 5 or ≥ 95.

## 3. The path after entry: can a bad trade be seen early, and cut?

**Early warning.** For trades still open k = 4, 8, 13 and 26 decision weeks after entry: "deeper" = |z| at week k at least
0.5 beyond |z| at entry; "recovering" = |z| at week k below |z| at entry; "in between" otherwise. Per state: trades, share
that end good, mean final extra; and what share of the trades that end bad were "deeper" at week k against what share
of those that end good.

**Exits (through the engine's book).** Rule R with one added exit, back to 50/50 at the next session:
- *z-stop*: when z reaches ±2, ±2.5 or ±3 on the held side (the stretch kept growing);
- *time stop*: after 13, 26 or 52 decision weeks in the trade.

After an exit the sleeve waits for a fresh first cross (z must come back inside the band and cross it again). The desk
removed T02/T03's time limit on 2026-11-17; the time stop here is description only.

## 4. Waiting for the turn (entry variants, through the engine's book)

- *re-cross*: from 50/50, enter when z comes back inside the band: z ≥ −1 this week after z < −1 the week before → hold
  the first leg; z ≤ +1 after z > +1 → hold the second leg. Exit at 0 as Rule R.
- *rebound*: from 50/50, once z has crossed beyond ±1, enter when it has come back 0.5 from its most extreme value since
  that cross (still on the stretched side of 0). If z gets back to 0 first, nothing is entered. Exit at 0.

For every variant of §3 and §4, per pair and window: closed trades, share worked, mean extra, CAGR, excess CAGR over 50/50
bought once (whole window and 2016–2026), max drawdown; for the six index pairs, how many are ahead of Rule R and of
50/50. With no added exit and the base entry, the state machine must equal `variants.rule_states` on every pair and
window (checked).

## 5. Can good trades be told apart before entry? A walk-forward test

- **Features:** E06's 24 conditions, §2's seven measures, and the z window's length in weeks (log).
- **Models:** a logistic regression (features standardised on the training trades, missing values set to the training
  median, L2 penalty C = 1) and a small gradient-boosted tree model (scikit-learn `HistGradientBoostingClassifier`,
  depth 3, 100 iterations, learning rate 0.05, seed 0) as a non-linear check.
- **Point in time:** for each calendar year Y from 2010 to 2026, each model is trained on the index-pair trades whose
  exit trade is before 1 January Y (counted and asserted) and predicts the index-pair trades entered in Y. All windows
  are pooled; the trades of different windows overlap, so the effective number of independent trades is smaller.
- **Score:** the AUC of the predictions against good/bad over all predicted trades; the mean extra and share worked of
  trades predicted above the training median against those below.
- **Against chance:** the whole walk-forward rerun with the good/bad labels shuffled within each (pair, window), 200
  times for the logistic model and 100 for the tree model (seed `default_rng([121, model_id, draw])`); the real AUC's
  percentile among them.
- **Gold pairs:** the index-trained models applied to the gold-pair trades of the same years (a different asset).
- **As an entry filter:** each year's models predict at every decision week of that year; the rule enters on a first
  cross only where the prediction is at or above the training median (all entries allowed before 2010). Through the
  engine's book, per index pair and window, excess CAGR over 50/50 from 2010-01-01 against the rule with every entry and
  against 200 filters that allow an entry at random at the model filter's own rate (seed
  `default_rng([122, model_id, pair_id, window_id, draw])`; the rate of each random filter is measured).

## Limits

- The same history that refuted the rule, searched again from new angles: §2–§4 look at many measures and variants, so
  some will look good by chance; §5 is the honest check, and even it uses design choices made after the history was seen.
- The windows' trades overlap; pooled counts overstate independent evidence.
- 2016–2026 is regime-heavy; gold is a futures continuation with an unconfirmed roll on 2026-01-30 (F-RA-D-OPEN-2);
  Micro250 pairs have four years.

## How to read it (2026-11-19)

Every number is in `out/E07_results.md` and on the page, printed by the script. Index pairs unless stated; trades
from 2006 (Micro250 pairs from 2022) to 2026-08-28.

1. **At entry, good and bad trades look the same; afterwards they part quickly.**
   - With the 52-week window, |z| at entry is 1.24 for good trades and 1.18 for bad ones.
   - Good trades were held a median 11 weeks and bad ones 57.
   - Bad trades kept stretching: the deepest |z| reached while open was 2.91, against 1.71 for good trades.
   - The same pattern holds in every window. The two groups' paths separate within the first 5–10 weeks.
2. **A bad trade can often be seen within two or three months.**
   - 52-week window, 13 weeks after the signal: of the 25 trades whose z had moved at least 0.5 further from the mean,
     20% ended good (mean extra −12.9%). Of the 24 whose z was closer to the mean than at entry, 67% did.
   - 61% of the trades that ended bad had already moved deeper by then, against 20% of those that ended good.
   - 26-week window, week 8: deeper trades ended good 32% of the time, recovering ones 66%.
   - A trade still open at week 26 mostly ends bad whatever z is doing.
3. **The ratio's character before entry says little, and not what was expected.**
   - The year-by-year look found the rule wins in years when the ratio moves little. That is something only known
     afterwards: measured beforehand, a ratio that had been swinging back and forth did not give better trades.
   - Pooled over the windows, a ratio whose z had changed sign more often in the past two years gave *worse* trades
     (rank correlation −0.07, percentile 0.1; negative before and after 2016; gold −0.12).
   - A ratio whose moves had been continuing gave slightly *better* trades: variance ratio +0.09 (percentile 100.0),
     autocorrelation +0.06 (97.7), 26-week efficiency +0.05 (97.2).
   - All these correlations are small, and most single windows are not notable.
4. **Stops cut the losses but do not rescue the rule.**
   - **z-stop at ±2 (the best variant).** It beat Rule R in 34 of 42 index pair-windows over the whole period and 33
     from 2016, a median gain of 0.81 pp a year from 2016. Fewer of its trades worked (51% against 66%): it gives up
     some trades that would have recovered to cut the long losers.
   - **13-week time stop.** It beat Rule R in 29 and 33 of 42, a median gain of 0.51 pp from 2016.
   - **Wider stops (±3, 52 weeks) and the waiting entries** gained little or nothing (the re-cross: median −0.09 pp from
     2016).
   - **None makes the rule a winner.** Every variant still trails 50/50: the best is ahead in at most 4 of 42
     pair-windows, with a median excess of −1.51 pp a year from 2016 (Rule R: −2.19 pp).
5. **Good trades cannot be picked before entry.**
   - Trained only on trades already closed and rating each next year's trades (2010–2026, 1,614 trades), the logistic
     model scored AUC 0.465 and the tree model 0.481. On shuffled labels the median was 0.50, so the real models sit
     at the 1st and 5th percentiles: slightly *worse* than chance.
   - The same models fit the past well (in-sample AUC 0.64 and 0.91). Patterns that sorted earlier trades pointed the
     wrong way later (for the logistic model, AUC under 0.41 in 2012, 2018–2019 and 2022–2023).
   - Trades the logistic model rated above its median did worse (mean −1.3%) than those below (0.0%).
   - As entry filters, the models beat the rule with every entry in 25–27 of 42 pair-windows, because trading less
     helps a rule that trails 50/50. Against random skipping at the same rate they were above the median in only 15–16
     (median percentile 29–39), and ahead of 50/50 in 1–2.
6. **Plain reading.** The rule's trouble is not picking the wrong entries; at entry, nothing found here separates a
   good trade from a bad one. Its trouble is long one-sided runs of the ratio after entry. Cutting a trade that keeps
   stretching (z beyond ±2) or lingers (over 13 weeks) removes part of the damage, about half to one point a year in
   most pair-windows, but not enough to beat holding both indices.
7. **If anything is worth testing:** "Rule R with a z-stop at ±2" is a new rule. It would need its own pre-registered
   test (T04), with the stop fixed in advance, its null, and data after 2026-09-08 as the hold-out.

- **Section 1's paths** use the legs' own closes. They describe how the ratio and the two legs moved, so a gold trade's
  path is gold's, while its good/bad label uses what was held.
- **Checks:**
  - the check against T03's evidence becomes a check against the engine on the same data (the decisions, and the book
    with the switch off);
  - the base variant must still give Rule R's NAV on every pair and window, now with the switch on the gold pairs;
  - the trades and conditions must equal E06's, which is re-run first.
- **Seeds, shuffles, models and thresholds** are unchanged.
