# E06 — Trade by trade: when did the ratio switch work? A search with an out-of-sample check (EXPLORATORY)

**Status:** EXPLORATORY and a **search**: description of the past, not a finding, not a test of any rule. Searching many
conditions on trades whose outcome is known will always find some that look good; this study is built to show how much
of that survives a check on data the search did not use. Anything that survives is a candidate for a new pre-registered
test (the next free T number, T04) with a true hold-out (data after 2026-09-08) — never a rule on its own.
**Asked:** 2026-11-18 — "examine all the trades and all the windows and look at the trades individually. perform
analysis and try to find conditions where strategy has actually worked".
**Script:** `python explore/E06_trade_search/search.py` → `explore/E06_trade_search/out/`
**Data:** the approved data of 2026-11-12, only through `analysis/approved_data.load_daily()`, on each pair's common
calendar and decision weeks (`analysis.ratio_engine.pair_frame`); index pairs from 2006-01-02, gold pairs from 2015-01-12,
Micro250 pairs from 2022-01-10, to 2026-08-28.

## The trades

Rule R (T03's rule) on every z window of the dropdowns (52, 26, 13 weeks on weekly closes; 250, 120, 60, 20 days on daily
closes read at the decision day), extreme ±1, all ten pairs, through the engine's book (`explore/E01_ratio_cycles/variants.py`;
the 52-week run is T03, checked). Every trade is kept, closed or open.

**Outcome of a trade:** the **extra** = the held leg's fill-to-fill return minus the peer's (E03's measure, before
costs); **worked** = extra > 0. Also shown: the trade's **piece** = the change of ln(strategy ÷ 50/50) over the holding
period, costs included (E04's measure); weeks and days held.

## The conditions (all known at the entry decision's close; nothing during the trade is searched)

Oriented to the leg the rule holds (the first leg when z < 0, the second when z > 0):

| key | condition |
|---|---|
| z_depth | \|z\| at entry (the chosen z window) |
| zslope4 | \|z\| now minus \|z\| four decision weeks earlier — how fast the stretch built |
| since_zero | decision weeks since z last changed sign |
| band_width | 2 sd ÷ mean of the window's closes |
| breadth | share of the ten ratios whose own z (same window) is beyond ±1 |
| lag4, lag13 | held leg's return minus the peer's over the last 4 / 13 decision weeks |
| held13, peer13 | each leg's own 13-week return |
| held52, peer52 | each leg's own 52-week return |
| trend52, trend104 | the ratio's log change over 52 / 104 decision weeks, signed in the held leg's favour (positive = the held leg has outperformed over the period, so the recent lag is a dip inside a longer lead) |
| long_cheap | the held leg's cheapness on a 156-week z of the ratio (positive = the held leg is also cheap against its 3-year average) |
| corr26, corr52 | the legs' trailing correlation of weekly log returns |
| corr52_chg13 | the 52-week correlation now minus 13 decision weeks earlier |
| vol_held, vol_peer, vol_ratio | 26-week volatility (annualised) of each leg and of the ratio |
| mkt52, mkt_dd, mkt_ma200 | N100's 52-week return, its drawdown from its highest close so far, and its close against its 200-day average |
| held_first | 1 if the held leg is the pair's first leg (the larger index; the index in a gold pair), else 0 |

The conditions shared with E03 must equal E03's values for every trade (checked against
`explore/E03_trade_conditions/out/trades_conditions_windows.csv`). The condition frame, recomputed from each pair's
history cut at 12 dates, must equal the full one up to each cut (breadth excepted: it is built from the ten z series, each
checked in E01/E03).

## Analysis

1. **All the trades, individually.** A table of every trade (window, pair, dates, leg, outcome, every condition) that can
   be filtered and sorted, and a chart of the ratio and z around any chosen trade. Per window: the spread of outcomes, the
   biggest winners and losers.
2. **Condition by condition.** Per z window, index pairs pooled: the rank correlation of each condition with the extra,
   both ranked within each pair (so a pair's level does not drive it), against 2,000 shuffles of the extras within each
   pair (seed `default_rng([108, window_id, condition_id])`); the mean extra and share worked in the condition's low,
   middle and high third (thirds within each pair). The same correlation on the entries before 2016 and from 2016 on, to
   see whether an association holds in both halves.
3. **The search.** Index pairs, all seven windows pooled.
   - *Discovery sample* = trades entered before 2016-01-01; *validation sample* = trades entered from 2016-01-01
     (the Micro250 pairs, from 2022, are validation only).
   - *Candidate rules:* each numeric condition at its discovery thirds and median (condition ≤ t or > t: six rules each);
     held_first = 1 or 0; and every pair of two such rules on different conditions (both must hold). A rule must select at
     least 15% of the discovery trades and at least 40 of them.
   - *Score:* the mean extra of the trades the rule selects, in the discovery sample.
   - *How good the best rule looks by luck:* the extras are shuffled within each (pair, window) in the discovery sample
     1,000 times (seed `default_rng([109, draw])`), the whole search is rerun on each, and the best score is recorded; the
     real best score's percentile among those. A rule's discovery score is worth something only if it beats what the
     search finds in noise.
   - *Validation:* every rule's selected mean extra and share worked on the validation sample, against 1,000 shuffles
     within each (pair, window) there (seed `default_rng([110, draw])`); and the correlation, across rules, between the
     discovery score and the validation score (if the search found something real, good discovery rules stay good).
   - *Gold pairs:* every rule, with its index-pair thresholds, on the gold pairs' trades — a second out-of-sample check on
     different assets.
4. **The best discovery rules as filters.** The top three rules by discovery score (chosen by discovery alone, before the
   validation numbers are looked at) used as an entry filter: the rule enters only on a first cross where the rule holds
   (and, for contrast, only where it does not), through the engine's book, per index pair and window; excess CAGR over
   50/50 on the whole window and on 2016–2026 alone.

## Limits

- It is a search on the same history that refuted the rule: the validation half was not used to choose the rules, but the
  rule itself and the study design were settled after the whole history had been seen; only data after 2026-09-08 is a
  true hold-out.
- The seven windows' trades overlap (the same episode can be a trade in several windows), so pooled counts overstate the
  number of independent trades, and the within-(pair, window) shuffles are narrower than they should be. The validation
  half and the gold pairs are the stronger checks.
- 2016–2026 is one regime-heavy decade (2020, the small-cap run); 2006–2015 holds 2008. A condition can work in one decade
  for reasons that do not repeat.
- Gold: futures continuation with an unconfirmed roll on 2026-01-30 (F-RA-D-OPEN-2).

## Added after the first run (2026-11-18)

Everything above was written before the first run and is unchanged. The first full run showed that the rules that held up
after 2016 are built on a market-wide condition (N100's 52-week return), which puts the six pairs' trades in the same
few years, and that "rule holds beats rule fails" can come from trading less. Two checks were added, run on the top three
discovery rules (still chosen by discovery alone):

1. **Year by year.** For each rule, the selected trades against the other trades *of the same calendar year* (index pairs,
   all windows; years with at least 5 trades in both groups), and the share of the selected trades that fall in the three
   busiest years. A rule that picks good years rather than good trades shows up here.
2. **Against random skipping.** Each filter's 2016–2026 excess against 200 filters that allow an entry at random at the
   rule's own rate (seed `default_rng([111, rule, pair_id, window_id])`). Outputs `out/rules_by_year.csv` and
   `out/filters_vs_random.csv`.

## How to read it (2026-11-18)

Every number is in `out/E06_results.md` and on the page, printed by the script.

1. **The trades.** 2,830 closed trades over the seven windows and ten pairs. Index pairs: 65% worked, mean extra −0.6%.
   The length of the hold separates them almost completely: trades held 13 weeks or less worked 77% of the time (97–98%
   in the longer windows), trades held over 52 weeks 1%. That is hindsight — the length is known only at the exit — so the
   question is what, at entry, tells the two apart.
2. **Conditions at entry that line up in both halves of the history** (index pairs, all windows pooled, notable, the same
   sign before and after 2016):
   - *A strong year behind the trade:* N100's 52-week return, and each leg's own 52-week return, go with better trades
     (each notable in four of the seven windows: 26 and 13 weeks, 120 and 60 days).
   - *A fresh stretch:* the longer z had stayed on the same side of zero before the entry, the worse the trade.
   - *The longer view:* the more the held leg had *outperformed* over two years, the worse; the cheaper it was on a 3-year
     z, the better. Together with E04 (stretched ratios keep stretching over weeks), this reads as short-term momentum
     and longer-term reversal.
   Several other cells are notable in one half only (the speed of the stretch, band width, the 52-week correlation, which
   leg is held). On the gold pairs the market-strength conditions do not carry over.
3. **The search finds more than luck would — before 2016.** The best of 7,211 rules picked trades with a mean extra of
   +1.7% (79% worked), above every one of 1,000 searches on shuffled outcomes (their median best +0.9%). Discovery and
   validation scores correlate +0.26 across rules: something carries over, but weakly.
4. **What survived and what did not.** The best rule before 2016 (a large recent gain of the peer while the ratio's
   52-week trend is flat) failed afterwards (−0.9%, 58% worked, against −0.8% and 66% for all trades), and so did its
   relatives. The rules built on **a strong market — N100 up more than about 25% over the past year** — held: the second
   rule (that, plus a held leg with 26-week volatility at or below about 23%) picked trades with +1.3% and 78% worked
   after 2016 (percentile 100), and did better than the gold pairs' average as well. Used as an entry filter it beats its
   opposite in all six index pairs in every window from 2016, and ends 2016–2026 ahead of 50/50 in three to five of the
   six pairs per window, where the rule with every entry is ahead in none.
5. **But the evidence is a handful of bull markets.** 75% of that rule's trades fall in 2024, 2007 and 2021, and within
   the years it beats the other trades of the same year in 7 of 9. Against filters that skip entries at random at the
   same rate it sits above the random median in 33 of 42 pair-windows (median percentile 80) but is notable in only 9; the
   failed rule 1 is above the median in 31. Its edge over 50/50 in 2016–2026 is small (median about +0.1 to +0.5 pp a
   year across windows).
6. **Plain reading.** The switch worked when everything was rising — the lagging index caught up in strong markets —
   and when the stretch was fresh and the held leg was cheap on a longer view. It failed in weak or falling markets and in
   long one-sided runs of the ratio, which is where the long losing holds come from. That is a pattern of a few bull
   phases, not yet a rule.
7. **If it is worth testing:** "T03's rule, entering only when N100's 52-week return is above a pre-set level" is a new
   rule and needs its own pre-registered test (T04) with the level fixed in advance and data after 2026-09-08 as the
   hold-out. Note how few strong-market episodes a hold-out of a year or two can contain.
