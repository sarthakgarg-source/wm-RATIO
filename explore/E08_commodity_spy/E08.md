# E08 — Commodity ETFs against SPY: which relationship is best, and does the ratio switch work there? (EXPLORATORY)

**Status:** EXPLORATORY — description, not a finding, not a test of any rule. These ETF ÷ SPY pairs were fresh data
(no ratio result had been computed on them). The desk chose on 2026-11-19 to explore them rather than use them for a
pre-registered test, so after this study they are no longer unseen: a later test of the ratio switch needs other data.
**Asked:** 2026-11-19 — "please include the Commodity etf vs SPY analysis. include any of these which gives the best
relationship", the ETFs being the 13 of the desk's commodity-ETF comparison (a written comparison of strategy and sector
weights; it has no price data and no SPY comparison).
**Script:** `python explore/E08_commodity_spy/commodity.py` → `explore/E08_commodity_spy/out/`
**Data:** `analysis/approved_data.load_us_etf()` only (approved 2026-11-19, `data/DATA_REVIEW_US_ETF.md`): total return
(rule H4), each fund's launch period dropped (H5), fill = the close (H3: the source has closes only). SPY from
2010-01-04; ETFs from their H5 start: GCC 2010, FTGC 2015, COMT and PDBC 2016, BCI, BCD and COM 2019, CMDY and SDCI 2020
(the nine **main** ETFs, five or more years); HGER, NBCM and PIT 2024, CERY 2026 (**short**, reported only). To 2026-11-18.

## The pairs

Each ETF ÷ SPY on the pair's common days (`analysis.ratio_engine.pair_frame`), first leg = the ETF, second = SPY.
Decisions on the last trading day of each ISO week; trades fill at the next session's **close** (H3), 10.4 bps per side
(the same cost as the NSE studies, for comparability; US ETF spreads are usually lower), units held, daily NAV.

## 1. The relationship, ETF by ETF (description)

On each main ETF's whole usable period: the correlation of weekly log returns with SPY, the beta, the lowest and highest
rolling 52-week correlation; the ETF's return in SPY's five worst months; the ratio's character (E07's measures
computed over the whole period: mean-reversion half-life, z sign changes per year with the 52-week z, variance ratio of
4-week to weekly changes, lag-1 autocorrelation, path efficiency); the correlations of the ETFs with one another.

## 2. "The best relationship" — fixed before any result is seen

For the ratio switch, the useful relationship is a ratio that comes back to its mean. **Score** of each main ETF, on the
**first half** of its own usable period (split at the middle date): the average of its ranks among the nine on
(a) the half-life (shorter is better), (b) z sign changes per year with the 52-week z (more is better), (c) the variance
ratio (lower is better). The three best-scored ETFs are **the chosen ones**. The rule's results are then read on the
**second half**, chosen against the other six. The diversification view (the lowest correlation with SPY over the whole
period) is reported alongside but chooses nothing.

## 3. The ratio switch on every ETF and z window

Rule R (T03's rule: z beyond ±1, exit when z is back through 0, no maximum hold) on each ETF ÷ SPY, for every z window
of the dropdowns (52, 26, 13 weeks on weekly closes; 250, 120, 60, 20 days on daily closes read at the decision day),
through the engine's book (`explore/E01_ratio_cycles/variants.py`). Per pair and window: closed trades, share worked,
mean extra (held − other, fill to fill), CAGR, excess CAGR over 50/50 bought once, max drawdown — whole period and each
half. **Against random timing:** T03's two nulls through the same book (`analysis.ratio_engine.null_A` — the blocks of
trade spells and the gaps permuted; `null_B` — each block's legs flipped), 500 draws each, seed
`default_rng([pair_id (+100 for N-B), 0, draw, window_id])`; every draw's counts are measured (V3-style). Percentile of
the real CAGR among the draws.

## 4. Equity curves

Per ETF and window: the value of 1 in the rule, the ETF, SPY and 50/50 bought once, with drawdowns.

## Checks

- Every pair frame's closes equal `load_us_etf()`'s (total return), and the calendar is the two legs' common days.
- The z series and the §1–§2 measures pass a cut-history check (recomputed from the history cut at 8 dates per pair).
- Every null draw keeps its counts (spells, switches, weeks per state for N-A; spells, switches, 50/50 weeks for N-B).

## Limits

- Few years: 6.9–16.9 per main ETF, so few trades with the long windows; one regime (2010–2026) for SPY.
- The nine ETFs are all broad commodity baskets and move together; they are not nine independent tests.
- Fills at the close, not OHLC4 (F-RA-D13); an unconfirmed spot check against issuer data (open item).

## How to read it (2026-11-19)

Every number is in `out/E08_results.md` and on the page, printed by the script. Main ETFs unless stated; periods from
each ETF's start (2010 to 2020) to 2026-11-18.

1. **As a relationship, the commodity ETFs diversify SPY, and COM most of all.**
   - Weekly returns correlate with SPY at only +0.25 to +0.42, with a beta of 0.13–0.39. The 52-week correlation swung
     between −0.29 and +0.89 over time.
   - In SPY's five worst months (SPY −9.3% to −9.5% on average), the ETFs fell −2.1% to −6.8%.
   - **COM** has the loosest tie: correlation +0.25, beta 0.13, and −2.1% in SPY's worst months.
   - The ETFs correlate 0.70–0.98 with one another (0.83–0.98 leaving COM out), so they are close to one asset, not
     nine.
2. **But they returned far less than SPY.** From 2010, GCC made +1.8% a year against SPY's +13.6%. The others made
   +4.5% to +10.9% against SPY's +13.1% to +16.2% over the same periods. Only SDCI (+16.4% against +14.1%, from 2020)
   did better.
3. **Each ETF ÷ SPY ratio behaves almost like a random walk.**
   - Variance ratios are 0.93–1.02 and autocorrelations −0.03 to +0.03.
   - Mean-reversion half-lives are 43–418 weeks, so there is little pull back to the mean.
4. **The "best relationship" chosen in advance did worse, not better.**
   - The score fixed before the run picked COM, COMT and BCI, because their ratios reverted fastest in the first half.
   - On the second halves, the rule's median excess over 50/50 was −2.42 pp a year for them, ahead in 3 of 21
     ETF-windows. For the other six it was −0.45 pp, ahead in 14 of 42.
   - Mean reversion seen in one half did not carry into the next.
5. **The ratio switch beat 50/50 in 18 of 63 ETF-windows, but not random timing.**
   - On the Indian index pairs the count was 0 of 42.
   - The wins are mostly the long windows on the ETFs that start in 2019–2020 (BCI, COM, CMDY, SDCI). Those periods
     contain one commodity boom: the rule held the ETF while it was cheap against SPY in 2020, and the 2021–2022 rally
     paid off.
   - Against the same trades placed at random times (N-A), only 2 of 63 were notably better: COM at 26 weeks and at 120
     days, percentiles 95.4 and 98.4. That is about what chance gives (about 3 in 63), and none was notably worse.
   - Against flipped legs (N-B), 1 of 63 was notable.
6. **Plain reading.** Commodity ETFs are a diversifier for SPY, COM the most, but over 2010–2026 they cost a lot of
   return. The z-score switch between a commodity ETF and SPY did not time the two better than chance. Where it beat
   50/50, it caught one commodity boom.
7. **These pairs are now explored.** Testing the switch here again would need data the study has not seen, such as
   the months after 2026-11-18 or other commodity series.

### How to read the gold addition (2026-11-20)

The paragraph above was written before the run and is unchanged. A trial run with 20 draws checked the code. The full
run (500 draws) left all the thirteen ETFs' rows unchanged (`rule_by_window.csv`, `trades.csv` and `relationship.csv`
compared column by column). Every number is in `out/E08_results.md` and on the page. GLD ÷ SPY from 2010-01-04 to
2026-11-18:

1. **As a relationship, gold diversifies SPY more than any commodity ETF.**
   - The weekly correlation with SPY is +0.14 (52-week range −0.45 to +0.58) and the beta 0.14. The loosest commodity
     ETF, COM, is at +0.25.
   - In SPY's five worst months (SPY −9.5% on average), GLD made −0.4%.
   - GLD correlates only +0.22 to +0.48 with the nine commodity ETFs, so it is a different asset from them.
2. **It also returned much less than SPY:** +7.0% a year against +13.6%.
3. **The GLD ÷ SPY ratio behaves like a random walk,** as the ETF ratios do: half-life 223 weeks, variance ratio 0.99,
   autocorrelation −0.00.
4. **The ratio switch did not beat 50/50 in any window.**
   - It was behind 50/50 bought once in all 7 windows: −0.36 pp a year (120 days) to −4.49 pp (20 days), median −1.43 pp.
   - Against random timing (N-A) its percentiles are 32.4–80.8, and against flipped legs (N-B) 27.4–69.2. None is
     notable.
5. **Plain reading.** Gold is the better diversifier for SPY, but switching between gold and SPY on the ratio's z-score
   did not add anything over holding both.
