## Part A — forward returns of the cheaper and the richer leg, by z-score

- **z** is E01's: (plain ratio − mean of the last 52 weekly closes) ÷ their sd — the rule's band in units of its own sd.
- **The cheaper leg** is the one that has lagged: leg a when z < 0 (the ratio a ÷ b is below its mean), leg b when z > 0.
  The other is the richer leg. This is also the leg the rule holds once |z| > 1.
- **Buckets:** z < −2, −2 to −1, −1 to 0, 0 to 1, 1 to 2, > 2 — and all weeks together.
- **Forward returns:** from the decision week's close to the close 4, 13 and 26 weeks later: the cheaper leg's, the
  richer leg's, their difference (cheaper − richer, the "spread": what the rule earns gross of costs if it holds the
  cheaper leg), and the average of the two (what the market did). Simple returns, no costs, no fills.
- **Per bucket and horizon:** the number of weeks, the number of episodes (runs of consecutive weeks in the bucket —
  the forward windows overlap, so the episodes are the nearer count of independent observations), the mean and the
  median of each return.
- **Against the random walks:** E01's 2,000 random paths of the ratio per pair (the same signs, seed
  `default_rng([101, pair_id])`; the point-in-time residuals with random signs plus the expanding drift, after the real
  52-week warm-up). The two legs' *common* movement is kept real: with M = ½(ln a + ln b), the random legs are
  a* = exp(M + ½ ln r*) and b* = exp(M − ½ ln r*), so only their relative movement is random. The same statistics on
  every path; percentile = (paths below + ½ ties) ÷ paths; notable = ≤ 5 or ≥ 95.
- **Point-in-time:** z at week t uses weeks up to t. The forward returns are, by construction, the future from t; the
  profile describes the history, it is not a signal.

## Part B — the market's condition at each trade, and how the trade did

- **Trades:** every closed round trip of T03 at window 52, exit E1, 20.8 bps — no maximum hold, so each trade lasts until
  the ratio is back at its trailing mean (open trades are left out of the outcome statistics and shown on the length
  chart). **Outcome:** the per-trade "extra" (the held leg's fill-to-fill return minus the peer's, no cost), the held
  leg's own return, the weeks and calendar days held. **Good** = extra > 0.
- **Conditions at entry** (at the entry decision's close, from data up to that day only; checked by recomputing every
  condition series from the history cut at 12 dates per pair, counted in audit.json):
  1. *Correlation between the legs:* trailing 26- and 52-week Pearson correlation of the legs' weekly log returns.
  2. *Market regime:* N100's trailing 52-week return (N100 is the market for every pair, gold pairs included) and its
     drawdown from its highest close so far.
  3. *Volatility and band width:* the held leg's and the peer's trailing 26-week volatility (annualised), the ratio's,
     and the band's relative width (2 × sd ÷ mean of the trailing 52 weekly ratios, in %).
  4. *Entry depth and speed:* |z| at entry; weeks since z last had the other sign; the held leg's return minus the
     peer's over the 4 and 13 weeks before entry (how fast it lagged); each leg's own 13-week return; the share of the
     ten ratios outside their own band that week (breadth); the calendar month (descriptive only).
- **Conditions during the trade** (concurrent — knowable only afterwards, labelled so): N100's return from entry fill
  to exit fill; the legs' correlation over the weeks held (4 weeks or more, else blank); the ratio's volatility over them.
- **Pools:** the six index pairs together and the four gold pairs together. Within each pool the trades are ranked
  within their pair before pooling, so a pair's level (a gold pair's volatility, say) does not drive the pooled number.
- **Two statistics per condition and pool:** (i) the median of the condition among good trades minus among bad trades;
  (ii) the rank correlation (Spearman) between the condition and the extra.
- **Null:** permutation. The extras (or the good/bad labels) are shuffled among the trades *within each pair*, 2,000
  times, seed `default_rng([105, 0, pool_id])`; percentile of the real statistic among the shuffled ones; notable
  = ≤ 5 or ≥ 95 (a two-sided 10% rate by chance). The null asks only "is this condition associated with the outcome?";
  it says nothing about whether a filtered rule would beat random timing — that is a new test.
- **Multiplicity:** 18 conditions × 2 statistics × 2 pools = 72 percentiles, so about 7 are expected to be notable by
  chance; the page counts them.
- **Also shown:** the trades on a timeline over the two legs (rebased), good and bad coloured; every trade as a stem at its
  entry date whose height and label are the calendar days held, with the trades entered each year; the trades split by
  the length of the hold (up to 13 weeks, 14–52 weeks, over 52 weeks).

## Limits

- 4–34 closed trades per index pair; the conditions are many and correlated with each other.
- The "during" conditions are hindsight by definition and are shown to describe, not to select.
- The forward profiles overlap week to week; the episode count is the honest n.
- Gold: futures continuation with an unconfirmed roll on 2026-01-30 (F-RA-D-OPEN-2); gold pairs from 2015; Micro250
  pairs from 2022.
- Anything that looks usable becomes a pre-registered test (the next free T number) before it is believed.

## How to read the results (2026-11-17)

Every number is in `out/E03_results.md` and on the page, printed by the script.

1. **Short trades win, long trades lose.** Split by the length of the hold, nearly every trade that ends within 13 weeks
   beats its peer, the 14–52-week trades are roughly even, and every trade held over 52 weeks lost — the long holds carry
   the rule's large losses.
2. **The "during" conditions are partly a restatement of the outcome.** A long trade is one in which the ratio kept
   moving the wrong way, so it spans more market movement and diverging legs; those associations describe what a bad
   trade looks like, not what to look for at entry.
3. **The conditions at entry that line up with good trades are about speed and depth, not the market or correlation.**
   For the index pairs, a sharper lag of the held leg in the 4 and 13 weeks before entry and a larger |z| go with better
   trades; the legs' correlation at entry and the market's 52-week return do not. These are candidates, not findings.
4. **Part A: the cheaper leg does not catch up more than a random walk's would.** Where the spread differs from the
   random walks in the stretched buckets, it is mostly lower, not higher. SC100 ÷ Micro250's red cells inherit E01's
   caveat for that pair: its random walks carry the steep drift of its 2022 warm-up year.
5. **Checks.** One Part A cell recomputed with pandas, the trades' extras matched with the evidence file, the held leg
   checked to be the cheaper one on every trade, one 52-week correlation recomputed by hand, the permutation draws
   checked; all agreed.
