# E09 — While in a trade, how much did the held index beat its peer? (EXPLORATORY)

**Status:** EXPLORATORY — description of the past, not a finding, not a test of any rule.
**Asked:** 2026-11-20 — "in this full analysis we are comparing it with 50 50 sleeve. thats fine but the important thing
to see is while in trade how much extra did it gave then its peer".
**Script:** `python explore/E09_intrade_extra/intrade.py` → `explore/E09_intrade_extra/out/`
**Data:** `analysis/approved_data.load_daily()` (the NSE indices and MCX gold, approved 2026-11-12) and
`analysis/approved_data.load_us_etf()` (SPY and 13 commodity ETFs, approved 2026-11-19; total return, launch periods
dropped, fills at the close), on each pair's common calendar (`analysis.ratio_engine.pair_frame`).

## Why this measure

The sleeve's result against 50/50 mixes three things: what happens during trades, the 50/50 weeks between trades, and
costs. While a trade is open the sleeve holds one index instead of half of each, so its edge over 50/50 in those weeks
is half the difference between the two indices. The cleanest view of whether the signal picks the right index is that
difference itself: **the held index's return minus its peer's, from the entry fill to the exit fill** (E03's *extra*).

## The trades

Rule R (T03's rule: z beyond ±1, exit when z is back through 0, no maximum hold) on every z window (52, 26, 13 weeks on
weekly closes; 250, 120, 60, 20 days on daily closes read at the decision day), through the engine's book
(`explore/E01_ratio_cycles/variants.py`), in three universes:

- **Indian index pairs** (6): N100, MC100, SC100, Micro250 against each other — T02/T03's deciding pairs;
- **gold pairs** (4): each index against MCX gold;
- **commodity ETFs ÷ SPY** (13): E08's pairs (nine main, four short).

## Measures, per pair and window (closed trades; an open trade is shown separately, to date)

- trades; share where the held index beat its peer; mean and median extra per trade;
- **compounded in-trade extra**: Π (1 + held return) ÷ (1 + peer return) − 1 over the closed trades — what holding the
  chosen index instead of its peer through every trade was worth;
- time in trades (years, and share of the period); **in-trade extra a year** = (1 + compounded) ^ (1 ÷ years in trade) − 1;
- by holding length (13 weeks or less, 14–52, over 52): trades, share won, mean extra.

Extras are before costs. A round trip moves the sleeve twice, about 0.2% of the sleeve at 10.4 bps per side.

## Against chance

T03's two nulls on the same targets (`analysis.ratio_engine.null_A`: the blocks of trade spells and the gaps permuted, so
the same trades at random times; `null_B`: each block's legs flipped by a coin, so the same timing with a random choice
of index), 1,000 draws each, seed `default_rng([universe_id, pair_id (+100 for N-B), draw, window_id])`. For each draw the
trades' extras are recomputed from the fill prices exactly as the engine's book would (a fast replay, checked equal to
the book's trades for the real targets on every pair and window), and every draw's counts are measured. Percentiles of
the real **mean extra per trade** and **compounded in-trade extra** among the draws; ≤ 5 or ≥ 95 = notable.

## Checks

- With the 52-week window the Indian and gold pairs' trades are T03's (`variants.check_against_t03`), and every universe's
  trades equal E06's / E08's where those exist (same entries, exits and extras).
- The fast replay equals the book's trades for the real targets everywhere.
- Every null draw keeps its counts.

## Limits

- The windows' trades overlap; the universes have different periods (Indian pairs from 2006, gold from 2015, Micro250
  and the short ETFs from 2022–2026).
- Extras before costs; fills at OHLC4 for the NSE/MCX legs and at the close for the US ETFs (F-RA-D13).
- The commodity ETF ÷ SPY pairs were explored in E08; the Indian pairs refuted the rule in T02/T03.

## How to read it (2026-11-20)

Everything above was written before the first run and is unchanged; a trial run with 20 draws checked the code. Every
number is in `out/E09_results.md` and on the page, printed by the script.

1. **The held index usually beats its peer, but the losers are much bigger.**
   - With T03's 52-week window on the six Indian index pairs (2006–2026, 101 closed trades), the held index beat its
     peer in 66% of trades, with a median extra of +2.1% per trade. The mean is −2.3%.
   - Per pair the held index won in 62–80% of trades (50% in SC100 ÷ Micro250).
   - The compounded in-trade extra is negative on all six pairs: −1.8% to −54.1%. N100 ÷ SC100 is −54.1% over 15.5 years
     in trades (−4.9% a year in trade); N100 ÷ MC100 −31.6% (−2.5% a year).
   - None of it is notable against the same trades at random times or with random legs (percentiles 8.1–59.7).
2. **The loss is entirely the long trades.** At 52 weeks:
   - trades over in 13 weeks or less: the held index won 98% of the time (43 trades), mean +3.6%;
   - 14–52 weeks: 68% won (37 trades), mean 0.0%;
   - over 52 weeks: 0% won (21 trades), mean −18.4%.
   - Every window shows the same split. The few long trades, about one in five at 52 weeks, wipe out the many quick wins.
3. **The sleeve is in a trade most of the time:** 73–79% of each Indian pair's period at 52 weeks. So the in-trade extra
   drives the result against 50/50; the sleeve earns half of it.
4. **Across windows**, the compounded in-trade extra is positive in 0–3 of the six Indian pairs, and the median per year
   in trade is −0.1% to −2.4%.
5. **The gold pairs are worse.** At 52 weeks the compounded extra is −34.5% to −80.7% (SC100 ÷ gold −80.7%), except
   N100 ÷ gold at +9.0%. Their trades held over 52 weeks lost a mean −41.6%. The open N100 ÷ gold trade since 2024-10-28
   stands at −98.5% to date.
6. **The commodity ETFs ÷ SPY are mixed and depend on the period.**
   - At 52 weeks, 4 of the 9 main ETFs are positive: BCI +30.5%, COM +37.5%, CMDY +24.1% and SDCI +20.2%. All four start
     in 2019–2020 and catch the 2021–2022 commodity boom.
   - The ETFs that start earlier are negative: GCC −47.7%, COMT −41.1%, PDBC −36.0%, FTGC −23.0%.
   - At 26 weeks and 120 days, 6 and 7 of 9 are positive (median +1.4% and +3.2% a year in trade).
   - In any one window, at most 1 of the 9 is notable against random times, which is chance level.
7. **Plain reading.** The rule's choice of index is right most of the time and pays in short trades. It loses because a
   minority of trades last over a year while the gap keeps widening. Seen this way, the problem is the hold, not the
   choice, which is the same conclusion E07 reached from the paths after entry.
