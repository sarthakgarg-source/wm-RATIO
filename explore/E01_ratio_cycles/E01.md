## The series

For each of the 10 pairs: the plain ratio r_t = P_a ÷ P_b on the last trading day of each ISO week (T02's series), and its
weekly change g_t = ln(r_t ÷ r_{t−1}) — the ratio's week-on-week change in per cent (continuously compounded). The weekly
change is the same whether the ratio is drawn plain or logged; the log/plain choice matters only for where a band sits
(T01 vs T02), not for cycles in the ratio. Before any statistic the average weekly change (the ratio's long-run drift) is
taken out: z_t = g_t − mean(g).

## The random-walk comparison (the null)

The question each analysis asks is "does the ratio do this more than a random walk would?" A random walk with the same
volatility is built by **flipping the sign of each week's change at random** (z*_t = s_t × z_t, s_t = ±1 with equal odds):
every week keeps its size, so volatility and its clustering (2008, 2020) are kept, but any link between one week and the
next — momentum, mean reversion, a cycle — is destroyed. 2,000 such paths per pair; seed `default_rng([101, pair_id])`
(pair_id = the pair's position in the engine's PAIRS list). Every statistic is computed the same way on the real series and
on each path; the result is the real series' percentile among the 2,000 (percentile = (paths below + ½ ties) ÷ paths ×
100). **Notable** = at or below the 5th or at or above the 95th percentile (outside the random walks' middle 90%).

## The four checks

1. **Multi-year (and shorter) cycles — the spectrum.** The periodogram of z shows how much of the ratio's weekly variance
   sits at each cycle length. A random walk's is flat; a cycle of period P shows as excess power near P; mean reversion
   at a horizon shows as a deficit at longer periods, trending as an excess. Power is summed in period bands of 2–4, 4–8,
   8–13, 13–26, 26–52, 52–104, 104–208 and 208–520 weeks, as a share of total variance. Only cycle lengths that fit at
   least 3 times in the pair's window are used (periods ≤ weeks ÷ 3: about 7 years for the index pairs, under 4 for the
   gold pairs, 1½ for the Micro250 pairs). One overall test per pair, so that trying 8 bands is not 8 chances: the largest
   |log(power ÷ the random walks' median power)| over the bands, against the same maximum in each random path.
   *No moving-average detrending anywhere*: smoothing a random walk with a moving average manufactures cycles of about the
   window's length (the Slutsky–Yule effect), which would make a 52-week band look cyclical by construction.
2. **Mean reversion by horizon — variance ratios.** VR(k) = variance of k-week changes ÷ (k × variance of 1-week changes),
   overlapping windows, for k = 2, 4, 8, 13, 26, 52, 104 weeks (only k ≤ weeks ÷ 4). Below 1: moves over k weeks tend to
   undo themselves (reversion, the swing-back the ±1 sd band bets on); above 1: they tend to continue (trend).
3. **Calendar seasonality.** The ratio's change over each calendar month (last common trading day to last common trading
   day), averaged by month of the year. Null: within each calendar year the twelve monthly changes are shuffled among the
   months (keeps every year's total; destroys any month-of-year pattern); 2,000 shuffles; seed `default_rng([104, pair_id])`.
   Overall test: the spread (sd) of the 12 monthly averages. Only pairs with at least 8 years in every month are tested
   (the Micro250 pairs, about 4½ years, are shown but not tested).
4. **Swing durations.** On the drift-removed ratio, a turning point is confirmed when the ratio reverses by at least one
   typical half-year move, h = sd(z) × √26, from its last extreme (a zig-zag filter; h is set per pair from its own
   volatility and applied unchanged to every random path). Measured: the number of completed swings, their median
   length in weeks, and how regular they are (coefficient of variation of the lengths). A real cycle would show as swings
   more regular than a random walk's (low CV); reversion as more, shorter swings; trending as fewer, longer ones.

## How to read it, and its limits

- About 10% of the percentiles will be notable by chance alone if every ratio were a random walk; the page counts how
  many are notable against that expectation. One notable cell is not a cycle.
- 20 years hold only ~3–7 repetitions of a 3–7 year cycle; the spectrum at those lengths is coarse, whatever it shows.
- The pairs share legs (N100 is in four), so their results are not independent.
- Gold: front-month futures continuation with an unconfirmed roll on 2026-01-30 (F-RA-D-OPEN-2); gold pairs from 2015.
- Price indices without dividends; both legs alike, so the ratio's cycles are unaffected except through the dividend gap.
- Anything that looks like a usable cycle becomes a pre-registered test of its own (a new T number) before it is believed.
