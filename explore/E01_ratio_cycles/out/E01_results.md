## Does the drift removal matter?

Both versions use only past data. **A** = the page: the drift removed at each week is the average of the weekly changes before it (expanding). **B** = no drift removal: the real ratio's raw weekly changes, against the same random walks with the expanding drift added back. Percentiles A → B; **bold** = the notable call changes; the spectrum column counts the cycle-length bands whose call changes (all bands are in drift_check.csv), with the overall p A → B. The month-of-year check never removes a drift.

**9 of 161** statistics change their notable call (variance ratios 8 of 62, swings 1 of 30, spectrum bands 0 of 69): SC100 ÷ Micro250 VR 2 wk (24.4 → 4.0); SC100 ÷ Micro250 VR 4 wk (19.3 → 0.7); SC100 ÷ Micro250 VR 8 wk (25.8 → 0.4); SC100 ÷ Micro250 VR 13 wk (59.2 → 2.2); SC100 ÷ Micro250 VR 26 wk (81.8 → 2.0); SC100 ÷ Micro250 swings: count (23.3 → 98.4); MC100 ÷ GoldMCX VR 13 wk (95.4 → 94.9); MC100 ÷ GoldMCX VR 52 wk (95.5 → 94.2); Micro250 ÷ GoldMCX VR 26 wk (95.0 → 93.7). Where A and B disagree on more than one call (SC100 ÷ Micro250) neither reading is reliable: the point-in-time drift starts from the warm-up year, and where that year differs sharply from what followed (the Micro250 pairs' warm-up is 2022) removing it leaves a slow trend in the residuals (A leans to 'continues') while adding it to the random walks leans B the other way.
