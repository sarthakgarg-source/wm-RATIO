# E04 — Does the ratio band have any predictive power? Outperformance, signal strength and the rule on random walks (EXPLORATORY)

**Status:** EXPLORATORY — description of the past, not a finding, not a test of any rule, never an input to one without a
new pre-registered test. Written after T03 (the ±1 sd first-touch switch on the plain ratio, no maximum hold) was locked,
run and refuted (C1 0, C2 2, C3 0 of 6); this asks *why* it fails and whether anything in the signal predicts.
**Asked:** 2026-11-17 — "in t03 can you check the outperformance. signal strength or is there any predictive power"
**Script:** `python explore/E04_signal_strength/strength.py` → `explore/E04_signal_strength/out/`
**Data:** the approved data of 2026-11-12, only through `analysis/approved_data.load_daily()`; the pairs' weekly closes on
their decision days (`analysis.ratio_engine.pair_frame`); T03's evidence (`tests/T03/evidence/headline/`: the daily NAV
files of the reference setting, `trade_extras.csv`, `decisions.csv`, `summary.csv`) at window 52, exit E1, 20.8 bps.
The NAV files are git-ignored and regenerated by `bash tests/T03/run.sh`.

## 1. Outperformance over time (T03's own book)

Per pair, from the first banded decision day to the last day: the strategy's daily NAV against 50/50 bought once
(the comparator T03 already reports). Shown: the relative NAV (strategy ÷ 50/50) as a line; calendar-year returns of each
and the excess (strategy − 50/50) per year; the share of years the strategy was ahead; the excess CAGR; the information
ratio (annualised mean ÷ annualised sd of the daily excess log return); the worst fall of the relative NAV. Description of
T03's path, no null (T03's N-A percentiles are the null for the CAGR).

## 2. Signal strength: what follows a band touch (event study)

- **The signal:** every first touch of the band — the first week the ratio is below mean − 1 sd (signal: hold a) or above
  mean + 1 sd (hold b), after a week that was not beyond the same side — whether or not the rule could trade it (all
  touches, so the signal is measured on its own, not through the rule's trade state).
- **What follows:** the cumulative relative return of the signalled leg against the other, ln(r_{t+k} ÷ r_t) for a touch
  below and −ln(r_{t+k} ÷ r_t) for a touch above, at k = 1 … 52 weeks after the touch (close to close, no cost). Averaged
  over touches, per pair and pooled over the six index pairs.
- **Against random walks:** E01's 2,000 random paths of each ratio (seed `default_rng([101, pair_id])`: the point-in-time
  weekly residuals with random signs plus the expanding drift, after the real 52-week warm-up — the paths E01 and E03
  use). The same touches and averages on every path; the band of the middle 90% and the percentile of the real average at
  each k. A random walk's touches have no reason to be followed by a swing back, so a real average above the band is
  the predictive power the rule bets on.

## 3. Predictive power: does the z-score rank future relative returns? (information coefficient)

Every week after the warm-up, the signal s_t = −z_t (positive = a is cheap) against the forward relative return
ln(r_{t+k} ÷ r_t), k = 1, 4, 13, 26, 52 weeks: the Spearman rank correlation (IC) per pair, and the average of the six
index pairs. Against the same random paths (the IC on every path; percentile). The forward windows overlap; the random
paths have the same overlap, so their spread is the honest yardstick. Also the IC in the first and the second half of each
pair's window (description of stability; no null).

## 4. The rule on random walks: are T03's trade statistics signal or mechanics?

The exact T03 rule (first touch of ±1 sd, exit when the ratio is back at its trailing 52-week mean, no maximum hold,
touches ignored while in a trade) run on every random path, and on the real ratio the same way: trades entered after the
warm-up, closed before the last week; each trade's relative return ln(r_exit ÷ r_entry) for a long-a trade, the negative
for long-b (decision close to decision close — the real trades are also recomputed this way and compared with T03's
fill-based extras). Per pair and pooled over the index pairs: the number of trades, the hit rate (relative return > 0),
the mean and the median relative return, the mean weeks held, the share of trades held over 52 weeks. Each real statistic
gets a percentile among the random paths. An exit at a *trailing* mean wins often on any series (the mean comes to the
ratio as well as the ratio to the mean), so a high hit rate means something only if it beats the random walks'.

## Counting and limits

- Percentiles: section 2 at 52 horizons (shown, not counted — neighbouring horizons are nearly the same number), section 3
  at 5 horizons × 10 pairs + 5 pooled, section 4 at 6 statistics × 10 pairs + 6 pooled; the page counts the notable ones
  (≤ 5 or ≥ 95) against the 10% expected by chance.
- The random walks keep each ratio's volatility, its clustering and its point-in-time drift; they remove any momentum,
  reversion or cycle — the property under question. They do not keep the two legs' common moves in the ratio (those
  cancel in a ratio anyway).
- Gold: futures continuation with an unconfirmed roll on 2026-01-30 (F-RA-D-OPEN-2); gold pairs from 2015; Micro250 from
  2022 (few touches and trades).
- Everything here is after the fact; a property found here becomes a pre-registered test before it is believed.

## How to read the results (2026-11-17)

Every number is in `out/E04_results.md` and on the page, printed by the script. The rule copy reproduced all of T03's
entries exactly, so the random walks ran the identical rule.

1. **No outperformance.** The strategy trails 50/50 bought once in every pair, by a few percentage points a year on the
   index pairs, with negative information ratios and fewer than half the calendar years ahead in most pairs.
2. **The band touch carries information — in the wrong direction.** After a first touch the signalled ("cheap") leg keeps
   doing worse than the other, more than on random walks; pooled over the index pairs the gap is largest around 26 weeks.
   The lagging leg keeps lagging: continuation, not the swing back the rule bets on.
3. **The z-score's rank correlation with the future relative return is at or below the random walks' at every horizon.**
   Read it against the grey band, not against zero: on a random walk a deviation from a *trailing* mean shows a positive
   rank correlation with long-horizon future changes in a finite sample (the known small-sample bias of long-horizon
   predictive regressions), so a real IC near zero at 26–52 weeks is already *less* swing-back than chance. The half-window
   ICs of the Micro250 pairs rest on about 70 overlapping weeks each and swing widely; they describe, they do not test.
4. **The rule's high hit rate is mechanics.** On random walks the identical rule wins slightly more often than on the
   real ratios, because an exit at a trailing mean also comes when the mean catches up. The real trades' average is below
   the random walks', and more of them run past a year.
5. **What this means for next work.** If the ratios have a usable property here it is continuation over 1–26 weeks, not
   reversion. A rule built on it (hold the leg that has led after a band touch) was suggested by this data and would be a
   new pre-registered test at the 98th–99th percentile, with costs; nothing here is evidence that it would pass.
